On May 25 Pope Leo XIV published **Magnifica Humanitas**, his first encyclical, devoted entirely to artificial intelligence. Given its reach, it is probably the statement on AI ethics with the largest potential audience in history: some 1.4 billion Catholics worldwide, about 500 million of them in Latin America. What is notable is that the document does not stop at principles: it lands on concrete policy proposals. You can read the official announcement on [Vatican News](https://www.vaticannews.va/es/papa/news/2026-05/papa-leon-xiv-primera-enciclica-magnifica-humanitas.html).

Three ideas will resonate most in the governance debate. First, a call for an international treaty banning lethal autonomous weapons systems, on the premise that no algorithm can make a war morally acceptable. Second, the explicit denunciation of "data colonialism" as a new form of exploitation between the North and the Global South, a framework that hits close to home for the region. And third, a striking proposal: that AI patents with high social impact be declared the **heritage of humanity**, with guaranteed universal access.

The Latin American angle is not a minor one. The [Latin American Episcopal Council (CELAM)](https://adn.celam.org/magnifica-humanitas-leon-xiv-en-el-tiempo-de-la-inteligencia-artificial/) reacted the same day, and media outlets and portals across the region (from [ACI Prensa](https://www.aciprensa.com/noticias/125373/el-papa-leon-xiv-publica-su-primera-enciclica-magnifica-humanitas) to the [Jesuit portal CPAL](https://jesuitas.lat/2026/05/25/primera-enciclica-del-papa-leon-xiv-magnifica-humanitas/) and [Página 12](https://www.pagina12.com.ar/2026/05/25/el-papa-leon-xiv-publico-magnifica-humanitas-su-primera-enciclica-que-dice)) quickly picked up its implications. One question remains open: which institutions in the region (bishops' conferences, ministries, multilateral bodies) could translate these principles into something operational.

## In the region

Brazil is this week the epicenter of AI governance in Latin America, with two moves in parallel. In addition to the TSE's electoral rules, the Chamber of Deputies has scheduled the floor vote on **PL 2338** for May 27; the bill aims to be the region's first AI legal framework with full national scope. It is not guaranteed: Senate Vice President Eduardo Gomes warned that the electoral calendar could push the discussion to 2027, while the Speaker of the Chamber, Hugo Motta, is sticking to the date. Tomorrow's outcome will determine whether the region gets its first comprehensive AI framework now or waits another year and a half.
