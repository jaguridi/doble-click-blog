## In 60 seconds

- **What happened.** As the UN debate opened on September 22, Lula called for regulating big tech and Trump rejected global control of AI.
- **Why it matters.** In our reading, the fact that Brazil and Argentina bring opposite answers on regulating AI takes weight away from a common regional position.
- **What we don't know yet.** What Javier Milei will say in his speech this September 23, in which, according to Infobae, he will call for AI not to be regulated.

Lula da Silva, the first speaker in the general debate of the 81st UN General Assembly, called the failure to take on the regulation of big tech and artificial intelligence an "omissão histórica imperdoável" (unforgivable historic omission), according to [the official text of his speech](https://gadebate.un.org/sites/default/files/gastatements/81/br_pt.pdf). He accused "a handful of techno-oligarchs," in his words, of promoting a race without ethical brakes, outside the UN and its Scientific Panel.

Hours later, Donald Trump said that the United States totally rejects any globalist scheme to control AI, and announced that his government's official documents will call it superintelligence. [PolitiFact](https://politifact.com/article/2026/sep/22/trump-un-speech-iran-ai-fact-check/) qualified his statements about AI: the United States leads China in model capability, but industry experts say China is only a few months behind, and "superintelligence" does not describe, according to the industry itself, the systems available today.

The divide also runs through the region. Javier Milei is scheduled to speak this September 23 and, according to [Infobae](https://www.infobae.com/politica/2026/09/21/los-ejes-del-discurso-que-dara-javier-milei-en-nueva-york-el-rol-de-la-onu-y-un-pedido-para-que-no-se-regule-la-ia/), will call for AI not to be regulated. In our judgment, the concrete effect is on the multilateral negotiation: if the UN moves ahead with a verification institution like the one some twenty countries called for, Brazil and Argentina will push in opposite directions and the region will not arrive as a bloc. There is a reasonable opposing reading: the speeches in the general debate do not oblige anyone to do anything, and the rules that do have effect are being written at home, as the cases of Brazil and Mexico below show.
