Anthropic confirmed on May 29 that its Mythos-class models (currently restricted to about 50 organizations under a closed program called Project Glasswing) will reach **all its customers "in the coming weeks."** It is the first documented AI system capable of autonomously identifying and exploiting security vulnerabilities, with a success rate above 83% on the first attempt in internal tests. The company announced it together with the close of a $65 billion funding round, according to [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/05/29/anthropic-raises-65-billion-at-record-965-billion-valuation-promises-mythos-ai-model-in-wide-release-in-coming-weeks-releases-claude-opus-4-8/).

The announcement brings a promise and a question. The promise: Anthropic says it has made "rapid progress" on stronger safety safeguards, and positions Claude Opus 4.8 (launched on May 28, with a fast mode three times cheaper and honesty improvements) as the bridge model toward that opening. The question is who validates those safeguards: no external evaluator or concrete date for safety validation was announced. For Latin America the point is even more direct. No country in the region is among the partners of Glasswing, the collective defense program that accompanied Mythos during its restricted phase. When the model opens up, the region will have access to the most advanced offensive system in existence without having taken part in the defense coordination built around it.

## In the region

The new Red Grooming LATAM guide (based on more than 28,000 anonymous surveys of children and adolescents in 14 countries) shows that the region lacks rules equivalent to those the European Union is already preparing against "nudification" tools. And the global rollout of Mythos arrives without any Latin American country having been at the defense table.
