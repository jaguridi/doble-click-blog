## In 60 seconds

- **What happened.** A UN General Assembly committee approved, in a split vote, a text supporting its scientific panel on AI and the Global Dialogue.
- **Why it matters.** Both bodies are already operating, but the political backing they receive will reach the plenary without consensus and with the European Union opposed.
- **What we don't know yet.** When the plenary will vote, and whether the delegations that abstained or set conditions will change their position there.

Draft A/C.2/81/L.2/Rev.1 was approved on November 24 in the Second Committee, according to the [UN record](https://press.un.org/en/2026/gaef3632.doc.htm). Russia introduced it. The text affirms the UN's universal convening role in the discussion of AI in the non-military domain. The United States said it sought to preserve the mandate of the co-chairs of the Global Dialogue on AI Governance. The European delegation explained its rejection: in its view, the text departs from a previous compromise. The record describes what the Assembly would do upon adopting it in plenary, so the committee does not have the final say.

Mexico voted in favor and called for respecting the mandates of the Independent International Scientific Panel on AI and of the Dialogue. Paraguay voted against. Colombia, Uruguay and El Salvador abstained. The panel has already released a thematic paper on how countries can build their own capacity, covered [on November 24](https://dobleclick.jaguridi.cl/en/2026/11/24/barbados-plazo-caricom-febrero-regular-ia-sola.html).

Mexico's condition suggests a concrete position for the plenary: supporting both bodies without reopening what the Assembly has already set on how they work. That delegation could put it in writing when explaining its vote. For Colombia, Uruguay and El Salvador, support for the panel appears to have become tied to a dispute over the text, not over the scientific work. If the plenary repeats the split, the panel would keep operating, but with contested political support.

## In the region

In Uruguay, the executive branch is preparing a decree on the use of AI in public administration, according to [Telenoche](https://www.telenoche.com.uy/nacionales/gobierno-prepara-un-decreto-regular-el-uso-la-inteligencia-artificial-el-estado-n5407725), which confirmed it with the digital government agency AGESIC. The draft requires each agency to inventory its systems, assess risks before deploying them and maintain human oversight.

In Brazil, rapporteur Aliel Machado told [Agência Brasil](https://agenciabrasil.ebc.com.br/politica/noticia/2026-11/projeto-de-regulacao-das-big-techs-sera-votado-na-proxima-semana) that the Chamber will vote next week on PL 4.675/2025 on digital markets. DPL News had reported on November 18 that the debate would take place on the 27th. The new text requires meeting at least four of six criteria to designate a group as systemically relevant, according to [Teletime](https://teletime.com.br/24/11/2026/empresas-de-internet-mantem-criticas-com-novo-texto-do-pl-4-675-2025/).
