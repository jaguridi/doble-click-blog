OpenAI opened free access to its frontier models for academic researchers for one year. [ChatGPT for Academic Researchers](https://openai.com/index/chatgpt-for-academic-researchers/) provides the equivalent of a $200-a-month Pro subscription (GPT-5.6 Sol Pro, Codex, expanded limits, long contexts) to a first cohort of 10,000 researchers that scales toward 100,000 in 2027, within a stated commitment of more than $250 million through that year.

What matters for the region is not the generosity but the fine print. To get in, three conditions must be met: being in a supported country, belonging to an accredited institution "with a high level of research activity," and having a recent preprint on arXiv, bioRxiv or ChemRxiv. These are exactly the three areas where Latin America is underrepresented, and the criterion for what counts as a research institution is defined by a private company from another country. A subsidy for scientific capacity distributed by geographic eligibility is development policy, whoever exercises it; the difference is that here no one voted for it.

It is worth being precise about what is not yet known: the full list of supported countries sits behind the application portal and could not be read directly. The regional exclusion is, for now, a reasonable hypothesis to be verified rather than a confirmed fact. The most useful way to settle it is for someone to apply from a Chilean, Colombian or Mexican university and report the result.

## In the region

Three fronts moved, and none of them is a new law. In Mexico, the UNAM admission exam crisis escalated to the president's morning press conference: Sheinbaum ruled out bad intent, attributed the problem to the lack of agreement on the use of the technology and of sufficient controls, offered federal support and asked the university to take action regarding the vendor Territorium Life; it emerged that there was one proctor for every 150 applicants. It is the first case in the region where the failure of an AI-mediated evaluation is being discussed as a problem of public procurement and vendor oversight, rather than of student honesty.
