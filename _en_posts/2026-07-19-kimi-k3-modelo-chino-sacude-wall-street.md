The launch of Kimi K3, the new open model from China's Moonshot AI, coincided with a historic drop in semiconductor stocks and put Wall Street on edge once again. According to [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/07/17/china-moonshot-kimi-k3-markets-china-ai/), the Philadelphia chip index lost as much as 5.7% in a single day and closed its worst week since April 2025 (‑11%), down 20% from its late-June peak. Amid the tremor, Apple briefly overtook Nvidia as the world's most valuable company.

Kimi K3 is a 2.8-trillion-parameter model that, according to Moonshot, outperforms Claude Fable 5 on programming tasks at a fraction of the price, and, because it is open, anyone can download and use it. It is the second time in eighteen months that a cheaper Chinese model has shaken the valuations of the entire US AI supply chain: the market is reading it as a replay of the "DeepSeek moment." For Latin America, the episode cuts both ways. On one hand, it validates the access argument: cheap open models lower the barrier to entry compared with the premium prices of US labs, something especially relevant for countries that are not going to build their own frontier models. On the other, the first public crack in confidence in the capital that sustains the global compute infrastructure is a reminder of how fragile the ground under this entire race can be.
