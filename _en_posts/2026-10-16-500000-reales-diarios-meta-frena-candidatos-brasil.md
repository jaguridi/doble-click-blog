## In 60 seconds

- **What happened.** A justice of Brazil's Superior Electoral Court barred Meta from restricting the reach of Facebook posts about the presidential candidates.
- **Why it matters.** In the final days of the campaign, when paying to boost political ads will no longer be allowed, the distribution Meta decides carries more weight for Lula and Flávio Bolsonaro.
- **What we don't know yet.** How the court will verify that Meta treats both candidates equally, and whether either campaign will ask for the fine to be applied before the vote.

The order covers three practices: reducing the distribution of posts about candidates, preventing them from being recommended, or limiting search results. Justice Marco Aurélio Bellizze of the Superior Electoral Court (TSE) signed it on October 15, according to [Agência Brasil](https://agenciabrasil.ebc.com.br/justica/noticia/2026-10/tse-determina-isonomia-apos-ia-da-meta-barrar-imagens-de-lula). It requires equal treatment for Lula and Flávio Bolsonaro, with a fine of 500,000 reais per day if Meta does not comply. The TSE website rejected the query on October 16, and the article does not identify the case file.

The case began with a complaint from Lula's campaign: Meta AI, the company's assistant, would not generate images of the president but did generate them of Flávio Bolsonaro. In his preliminary review, the justice concluded that the control over the instructions failed. He also said there is no evidence to claim it was a deliberate choice. Meta responded that it had temporarily prohibited recommending candidates. Bellizze had already ordered Jorge Messias, the Solicitor General of the Union, to remove an AI video favorable to Lula, according to [this October 14 entry](https://dobleclick.jaguridi.cl/en/2026/10/14/15000-becas-7-millones-licencias-colombia-google.html).

The calendar explains why the order could weigh more than an ordinary fine. Starting October 22, the law bans new synthetic content and paid boosting of political ads, and the runoff is on the 25th. In that final stretch, no campaign could make up for a drop in Facebook reach with paid ads. The threat of 500,000 reais a day would then be the main pressure on how Meta distributes each candidate.
