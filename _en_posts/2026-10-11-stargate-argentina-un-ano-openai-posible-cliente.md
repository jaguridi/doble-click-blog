## In 60 seconds

- **What happened.** Chequeado confirmed that Stargate Argentina, the data center announced with OpenAI in October 2025, shows no progress or concrete investment.
- **Why it matters.** OpenAI says it would buy capacity, but that the development would be led by Sur Energy, so the project depends on that local partner.
- **What we don't know yet.** Whether Sur Energy is moving ahead with the data center and who would put up the capital, questions the company had not answered publicly as of October 11.

Argentina's Ministry of Economy told [Chequeado](https://chequeado.com/el-explicador/stargate-argentina-a-un-ano-del-anuncio-no-hay-avances-en-la-construccion-del-data-center-de-us-25-000-millones-para-openai/) that there had been "no movement at all" to register the project under the RIGI, Argentina's incentive regime for large investments. The outlet published its fact-check on October 10, one year after the announcement. On that day in 2025, [the Argentine government reported](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/noticias/el-presidente-milei-recibio-representantes-de-openai-en-casa-rosada) that OpenAI and Sur Energy had signed a letter of intent. The document spoke of a facility of up to 500 megawatts and an investment of up to $25 billion. As of October 11, [Sur Energy's media center](https://www.sur.energy/media-center) showed no press releases after that signing.

An OpenAI spokesperson explained the company's role to the same outlet: it would be a possible buyer of compute capacity, while Sur Energy would lead the development. Sur Energy did not respond to Chequeado's inquiries.

That division suggests where the fragility of the announced figure lies. If OpenAI comes in as a customer, that investment would depend on Sur Energy bringing together those who would finance and build the facility. There is a regulatory path open: according to Chequeado, the Senate is considering the Super RIGI, which has already passed the Chamber of Deputies. That regime is designed for new sectors, including the installation of compute infrastructure, and requires investments of more than $1 billion. With that law in force, Stargate Argentina would have an option to apply for it, although that would not replace an investor either.
