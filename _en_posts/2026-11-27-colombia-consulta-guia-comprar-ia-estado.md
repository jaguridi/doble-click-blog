## In 60 seconds

- **What happened.** Colombia's public procurement agency put out for comment a draft that guides government entities on how to use and contract AI systems.
- **Why it matters.** Anyone selling AI to the Colombian government could have to agree by contract not to train models on the purchasing entity's data.
- **What we don't know yet.** What comments the agency will receive during the consultation and how much the draft will change before its final version is published.

An assessment by Colombia Compra Eficiente identified 54 AI-related contracts between 2020 and 2025, mostly awarded through direct contracting. The figure appears in the [draft guide](https://www.colombiacompra.gov.co/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Guia-para-el-Uso-y-Adquisicion-de-Soluciones-Tecnologicas-con-Inteligencia-Artificial-en-la-Contratacion-Estatal-V.1.0.pdf) that the agency, Colombia's National Public Procurement Agency, opened for comment on November 26. It runs 137 pages on principles, planning, selection, clauses and disclosure. The consultation closes on December 10, according to the [official call](https://www.colombiacompra.gov.co/archivos/convocatoria/participacion-ciudadana-del-borrador-de-la-guia-para-el-uso-y-adquisicion-de-soluciones-tecnologicas-con-inteligencia-artificial-en-la-contratacion-estatal).

According to the [draft](https://www.colombiacompra.gov.co/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Guia-para-el-Uso-y-Adquisicion-de-Soluciones-Tecnologicas-con-Inteligencia-Artificial-en-la-Contratacion-Estatal-V.1.0.pdf), the public servant who evaluates a bid or oversees a contract may rely on AI. That official does, however, retain the authority and the responsibility for what they decide. The text says that emotion recognition systems should not be acquired for the workplace or for educational institutions. It also calls for agreeing with the vendor that it will not use the entity's data to train models without express authorization.

That clause directly affects companies that sell AI to Colombian government entities. The guide presents it as something agreed in the contract, so its effect would depend on each entity including it when it buys. If it survives the consultation, vendors would face a reference text that entities could demand of them in negotiations. The predominance of direct contracting suggests that many of those purchases were agreed without comparing bids. In that scenario, what the contract says could carry more weight.

## In the region

In Argentina, on November 26 the Senate declined to take up "sobre tablas," that is, without first going through committee, a bill by Senator Alejandra Vigo. It amends the Penal Code to punish forms of digital violence. It includes the non-consensual creation and distribution of AI-generated nude images. The motion received 28 votes in favor and 43 against, short of the required two-thirds, according to [elDiarioAR](https://www.eldiarioar.com/politica/libertad-avanza-bloqueo-senado-tratamiento-proyecto-violencia-digital-mujeres-ninas_1_13697384.html). Patricia Bullrich, head of the La Libertad Avanza caucus, said the executive branch had sent another bill on protection in digital environments. She proposed taking up the issue in extraordinary sessions, without a firm commitment.
