## In 60 seconds

- **What happened.** The digital transformation authorities of the Ibero-American countries approved the Ibero-American AI Alliance in Madrid and referred it to the heads of state.
- **Why it matters.** Since each government decides whether to join, the real reach of the shared compute and data will depend on how many countries sign on and what they contribute.
- **What we don't know yet.** Which governments will formally join, and under what rules high-performance computing capacity will be divided among the participating projects.

Two footnotes say that Argentina and Paraguay dissociate themselves from the text. They appear in [the declaration of the ministerial meeting](https://digital.gob.es/content/dam/portal-mtdfp/comunicacion/comunicacion_ministro/2026/11/03-11-2026/Declaraci%C3%B3n%20de%20la%20Reuni%C3%B3n%20Ministerial%20de%20Transformaci%C3%B3n%20Digital%20e%20IA.pdf). The document describes three components. The first is an Ibero-American Data Space with federated architectures, in which each institution keeps its own data and the network connects them. The second is models in Spanish, Portuguese and, progressively, Indigenous languages. The third is cooperation on high-performance computing, the large servers needed to train homegrown models. Joining will be voluntary.

The 30th Ibero-American Summit closed on November 5 with the Alliance approved and with the two countries dissociated, according to [Infobae](https://www.infobae.com/espana/2026/11/05/la-xxx-cumbre-iberoamericana-cierra-una-declaracion-final-y-acuerdos-sobre-inteligencia-artificial-y-proteccion-civil/). The outlet adds that the initiative seeks to connect four language model projects: ALIA in Spain, AMALIA in Portugal, Latam-GPT in Chile and SoberanIA in Brazil. The Summit's final text was not available on the website of the Ibero-American General Secretariat as of November 7.

For Latam-GPT, the Chilean model on that list, the compute component could be the one that matters most. If the Alliance sets rules for access to the shared capacity, those rules could determine how much compute its next versions are trained with.
