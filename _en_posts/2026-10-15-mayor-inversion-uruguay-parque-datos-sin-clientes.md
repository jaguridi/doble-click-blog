## In 60 seconds

- **What happened.** On October 14, Uruguay signed a $5.7 billion agreement with Spain's Solaria for a 500-megawatt data center park.
- **Why it matters.** If the full park consumes more than its solar plant generates, the difference would have to come from the public grid operated by UTE.
- **What we don't know yet.** Who will use the park's capacity, because the official statement, reviewed on October 15, does not name any customer.

The money is split into two parts: $5.5 billion for data centers and $200 million to install 350 megawatts of its own solar generation. That is the breakdown given by [the Presidency of Uruguay](https://www.gub.uy/presidencia/comunicacion/noticias/presidente-orsi-anuncio-mayor-inversion-historia-del-pais-solaria), which presents the project as the largest investment the country will receive in its history. President Yamandú Orsi signed with Solaria Energía y Medio Ambiente. The facilities will be in Canelones, San José and Florida, and the park will operate as a free trade zone.

The schedule has two phases. The first brings together 200 megawatts of data centers and all of the planned solar generation. The second adds another 300 megawatts of data centers, with no new solar plant in the plan as described. Once the park is finished, the data centers' capacity would exceed the installed solar power by 150 megawatts.

That gap suggests where the project's least visible negotiation may lie. A park that needs electrical backup would have to connect to UTE, the state-owned company that operates Uruguay's grid. How much power it would draw from the system, and on what terms, could define the project's real weight for the public grid. The government estimates 3,100 construction jobs in the first stage and 1,500 permanent ones once the project is complete.
