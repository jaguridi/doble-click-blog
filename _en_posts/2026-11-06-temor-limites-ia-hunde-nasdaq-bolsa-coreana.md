## In 60 seconds

- **What happened.** The Nasdaq Composite fell 5.52% on November 5, its worst day since April 2025, and the next day South Korea halted its stock market.
- **Why it matters.** The firms that operate data centers lost more than Nvidia: the market seems to see more risk in local permits than in chips.
- **What we don't know yet.** What specific rules the new Congress and state governments will approve on permits and grid connections for data centers.

IREN, Applied Digital and Nebius, which operate data centers, each lost more than 14% on November 5. Nvidia, which sells the chips those centers run on, fell 9.26%. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index dropped more than 12%. The figures come from [Fox Business](https://www.foxbusiness.com/markets/stocks-nov-5-2026), which also reports that the Dow Jones lost 1,833.06 points (3.44%) and the S&P 500, 4.36%.

[NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/11/05/nx-s1-6031987/stock-markets-ai-midterm-elections) attributed the drop to investors' fear that the new Congress, with a Democratic majority, and the states will restrict AI and data centers. It estimated more than $2 trillion in lost market value. On November 3, voters in several cities had already rejected data centers in local ballot measures, according to the entry [from November 5](https://dobleclick.jaguridi.cl/en/2026/11/05/modelo-openai-rinde-menos-seguridad.html). On November 6, the Kospi, South Korea's main index, fell as much as 8.7%. The Korea Exchange then triggered a circuit breaker, the automatic trading halt in the face of sharp drops, according to [Nikkei Asia](https://asia.nikkei.com/business/markets/taiwan-stocks-in-steepest-ever-fall-south-korea-halts-trading-amid-ai-rout).

The gap between operators and manufacturers suggests where investors see the risk. A data center operator needs local permits and a connection to the power grid to grow, and those decisions go through states and municipalities. If that reading is correct, a state permitting rule could affect these companies as much as a federal law. The figures and the explanation of the drop come from the press, with no official documents linked.
