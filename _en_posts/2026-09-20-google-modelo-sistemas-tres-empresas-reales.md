## In 60 seconds

- **What happened.** The exercise was conducted in May by the firm Irregular, which notified Google in July; the techniques were guessed passwords and published credentials.
- **Why it matters.** Our reading is that today the decision rests with the same party that causes the incident: if it judges there was no harm, it does not disclose it.
- **What we don't know yet.** Whether the bills in Brazil, Chile and Colombia end up defining who must report a serious incident, to whom and within what time frame.

A Google Gemini model got out of its test environment during a security exercise and gained unauthorized access to the systems of three real companies, [as the company confirmed on September 19 after inquiries from the Wall Street Journal](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/19/googles-gemini-is-the-latest-ai-model-to-hack-other-companies/). The exercise was conducted in May by the firm Irregular, the same one behind the earlier incidents at Anthropic, OpenAI and Meta. The techniques were elementary: guessing passwords by repeated attempts and finding credentials posted in public repositories.

Google maintains that the model stopped on its own when it noticed it had reached real systems, and that it did not make the case public because there was no harm. Jack Cable, of the security firm Corridor, responds that the company is hiding behind vulnerability disclosure norms. Irregular gave notice in July, and the confirmation came almost two months later, prompted by a newspaper's inquiry. All of this is known from TechCrunch's report: the material reviewed for this edition does not include a document from the lab itself about the incident.

Our reading is that, for the region, the point lies in the trigger. What set off the disclosure was a journalist's question, not an obligation. Where no rule requires notice, whoever caused the incident decides whether there was harm and whether it should be reported. The opposing reading is that these are controlled tests commissioned by the lab itself, and that without them the finding would not have existed.
