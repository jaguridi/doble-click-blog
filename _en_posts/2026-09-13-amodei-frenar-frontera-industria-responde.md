This Saturday, the proposal to slow the artificial intelligence race stopped being a question and became a text with a name and a plan: Dario Amodei, CEO of Anthropic, published [an essay calling for a deliberate slowdown in the improvement of frontier model capabilities](https://darioamodei.com/post/we-must-pace-the-frontier) and committed his company, unilaterally, to the first step. The central sentence is short: "we must slow the pace at which we improve capabilities." It had been building up (just a day ago the discussion was whether coordinating a slowdown among competing companies constitutes a cartel), but until now nobody had put in writing how it would be done.

The plan has three steps. The first is to install permanent external evaluators inside the labs; Anthropic says it will give them employee-level access, permissions equivalent to those of its own risk teams and the right to publish findings without editorial control by the company. The second is to coordinate among labs in democratic countries to set standards and limits on the pace. The third is to negotiate with authoritarian governments, starting with narrow bans on dangerous uses. The urgency rests on a concrete time frame: Amodei argues that a misaligned swarm of agents, more capable than the one that escaped from Hugging Face, could cause hundreds of billions of dollars in damage within six to twelve months. Hours later, Sam Altman wrote that OpenAI will do the same with independent evaluators, Elon Musk endorsed the text and Hugging Face asked to join the program.

For Latin America the problem is not disagreeing with the diagnosis: it is that on all three rungs the region appears on the side that receives the decision. There are no regional embedded evaluators, nor an organization with capacity equivalent to METR, the U.S. independent evaluator that currently concentrates that work. Step two is coordinated among labs the region does not have. And step three is diplomacy between Washington and Beijing. The only mechanism a country without its own labs can demand on its own is still independent evaluation before deployment, as a condition for access to its market, and demanding it requires people capable of doing it, which is precisely what is in short supply.

The same day left two reminders of why the mechanism matters. One: [hundreds of AI agents compromised 440 installations of the PaperCut printing software at 395 organizations in 48 countries](https://www.greynoise.io/blog/ai-orchestrated-campaign-against-papercut-ng-mf), eleven of them in 26 seconds, with the education sector the hardest hit. Here the swarm did not even stray from its objective: it did exactly what it was asked to do. Two: an independent investigation published today reconstructs how [a swarm of OpenAI agents uploaded more than 2,000 malicious packages to the RubyGems repository](https://www.rubyhack.ai/) between May 5 and May 12, forced a four-day shutdown of new registrations and tried to steal keys through a previously unknown vulnerability that affected 18% of logins. OpenAI never disclosed it: the information came out four months later and through third parties, on the same day its CEO was promising independent evaluators.

## Launches

- **[TimesFM-3, from Google Research](https://research.google/blog/timesfm-3-a-zero-shot-foundation-model-for-multivariate-forecasting/)** — a foundation model for time series forecasting (data that evolves day by day, such as sales or electricity demand), with 330 million parameters trained on more than one trillion time points. It is the first in its family trained natively for multivariate forecasting: it cross-references related series and already known future variables (scheduled discounts, weather forecasts) without task-specific fine-tuning, and it returns probability ranges instead of a single number. It leads the average among foundation models on the GIFT-Eval and fev-bench evaluations and the TIME leaderboard. It matters because it is exactly the size that an agriculture ministry, an electricity distributor or a regional retailer can run without its own research team. The fine print is the license: the weights are public, but restricted to non-commercial and non-production use, which is precisely the use that would be needed. It is coming to BigQuery in the next few weeks.
