## In 60 seconds

- **What happened.** The panel is studying four measures, among them auditors inside the labs and an emergency shutdown to be checked continuously by third parties.
- **Why it matters.** Our reading: the four measures under study require access to the lab and the technical capacity to verify them, not just a law that classifies risks.
- **What we don't know yet.** The panel only recommends: it remains to be seen whether California turns those measures into obligations and who would be in charge of checking them.

California's governor signed [Executive Order N-9-26](https://www.gov.ca.gov/2026/09/18/governor-newsom-issues-executive-order-to-accelerate-independent-oversight-and-advance-the-creation-of-an-ai-kill-switch/) on September 18, convening a group of experts to recommend within two months how to strengthen the state's artificial intelligence safety laws. There are four measures under study: independent auditors embedded inside frontier labs, verified safety frameworks with transparency reports, an emergency shutdown whose effectiveness is checked continuously by independent organizations, and an expanded definition of critical incident that includes loss of control. Newsom justified the order on the grounds that no federal law requires dangerous incidents to be reported.

The embedded auditor already operates as a private initiative. Anthropic [named Accenture its first embedded evaluator](https://www.anthropic.com/news/accenture-embedded-evaluation), with employee-level access to the company, and Accenture also sells deployments of the same technology it would be evaluating. In the [September 17 entry](https://dobleclick.jaguridi.cl/en/2026/09/17/openai-escribe-reglas-contar-sus-fallas.html) we reported that this design came without the authority to halt a deployment. What California adds is the attempt to make it a public obligation, with a third party checking the shutdown on a continuous basis.

Our reading is that this is where the regional discussion shifts plane. Brazil's PL 2338/2023 and Chile's bill (boletín) 16.821-19 were built on classifying systems by risk level, work that gets settled when the text of the law is written, as [we summarized on August 26](https://dobleclick.jaguridi.cl/en/2026/08/26/primer-chip-openai-gana-nvidia-eficiencia.html). A verifiable shutdown and an auditor with inside access get settled somewhere else: they require getting into the lab and having someone who knows how to check what they see. The opposing reading is that the order does not yet oblige anyone to do anything, because the panel only recommends and the laws are written afterward.
