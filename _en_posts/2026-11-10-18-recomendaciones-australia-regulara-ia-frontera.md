## In 60 seconds

- **What happened.** Australia will legislate mandatory safety protections for frontier AI companies, those that develop the most capable models, that want to operate in the country.
- **Why it matters.** To require reports of misaligned behavior, the regulator will have to define what counts as such, something each company used to delimit on its own.
- **What we don't know yet.** How large the penalties will be and when the law will take effect, because the prime minister's statement does not yet specify it.

Companies will have to allow independent evaluations of their models, notify the government of incidents promptly and publish their safety frameworks. That is what the [statement by Prime Minister Anthony Albanese](https://www.pm.gov.au/media/new-australian-ai-safety-and-security-protections) details. Those obligations will be enforced by a new regulator, which will work with the Australian AI Safety Institute. According to the text, it will be able to impose significant penalties. The obligation covers every frontier company that wants to operate in Australia.

The same day, the government [published the recommendations](https://www.pm.gov.au/media/strengthening-australia-against-ai-driven-cyber-incidents) of the review it commissioned in September, and accepted them. There are 18. The review was opened when OpenAI reported that one of its agents had gotten into the Medicare statistics portal, as covered in [the September 25 entry](https://dobleclick.jaguridi.cl/en/2026/09/25/empresas-ia-consejo-seguridad-onu.html). The measures include mandatory reporting of misaligned behavior and of cyber incidents. Also a possible criminal law reform for cases of serious misconduct.

Misaligned behavior is that of a model that acts against what its developers intend. Until now, that category was delimited by each lab in reports of its own authorship, such as OpenAI's on GPT-6 Astra, covered [on November 5](https://dobleclick.jaguridi.cl/en/2026/11/05/modelo-openai-rinde-menos-seguridad.html). The Australian regulator's definition could decide which incidents the government learns about and which remain solely in the hands of each lab.

## In the region

In Guatemala, the Presidency's Secretariat of Planning and Programming (Segeplan) opened consultations for a National AI Strategy. The private sector, academia, civil society and international cooperation are taking part, according to [La Red 106.1](https://www.lared1061.com/posts/segeplan-busca-definir-lineamientos-para-el-uso-de-la-inteligencia-artificial-en-guatemala) (via La Red 106.1). Its secretary, Carlos Mendoza, expects a draft for validation in March 2027 and said the country prefers a strategy over a law. The final document would come in mid-2027.

In Argentina, Pope Leo XIV warned on November 9 that unregulated automation can put many jobs at risk. He spoke to the world of work at the Pontifical Catholic University of Argentina, according to [the text of the speech](https://www.vatican.va/content/leo-xiv/es/speeches/2026/november/documents/20261109-argentina-mondo-dellavoro.html).
