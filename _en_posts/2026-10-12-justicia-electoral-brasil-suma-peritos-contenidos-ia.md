## In 60 seconds

- **What happened.** Brazil's Superior Electoral Court created a Digital Forensics Unit to examine images, audio, and videos suspected of having been manipulated with AI.
- **Why it matters.** Campaigns litigating before the court will be able to contest a technical report with stated methods and limitations, not just a conclusion.
- **What we don't know yet.** What criteria the court will use to add universities and outside experts to its registry of specialists, a point its announcement does not detail.

Portaria-TSE nº 667/2026 appeared in the Electronic Journal of Electoral Justice on Thursday, October 8, according to [the Superior Electoral Court (TSE)](https://www.tse.jus.br/comunicacao/noticias/2026/Outubro/tse-cria-nucleo-de-forense-digital-para-reforcar-analise-de-conteudos-manipulados-por-ia). The unit will act on an exceptional basis, depending on the complexity, severity, scale, or potential for harm of each case. It is not an autonomous administrative unit, and the court's Judicial Secretariat coordinates requests for analysis. It can review files and metadata, as well as signs of automation, mass messaging, and coordinated inauthentic behavior, according to [CNN Brasil](https://www.cnnbrasil.com.br/eleicoes/tse-institui-nucleo-dedicado-a-analise-de-conteudos-manipulados-por-ia/). That list suggests the unit will be able to look at how a piece circulates, in addition to how it was made.

Automated tools play an auxiliary role: specialists interpret the results. Every expert report will have to set out the materials, methods, results, limitations, and the people responsible, according to the description [from the court](https://www.tse.jus.br/comunicacao/noticias/2026/Outubro/tse-cria-nucleo-de-forense-digital-para-reforcar-analise-de-conteudos-manipulados-por-ia). The ordinance also provides for a list of collaborating experts and institutions, subject to confidentiality rules.

That format could change the debate in the cases that reach the unit, because it makes visible how each conclusion was reached. A campaign's defense that objects to an expert report would have before it a document with its method and its limits in writing, which it can challenge point by point. At the same time, the exceptional use suggests that a large share of complaints about AI content will continue to be resolved without this analysis.
