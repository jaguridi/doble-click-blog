The World Artificial Intelligence Conference (WAIC) in Shanghai closed on July 20, confirming that the World AI Cooperation Organization (WAICO), the AI cooperation organization founded by China days earlier, is already operating with 29 member countries, among them Brazil, Cuba, Venezuela and Nicaragua, which will not be required to align with the European AI Act, the OECD principles or the G7 Hiroshima Process. The picture the week leaves behind, according to [coverage of the closing](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/320997/20260720/waic-ends-two-incompatible-ai-governance-orders-locked-enterprises.htm), is one of two governance orders that no longer merely compete but are starting to become technically incompatible with each other.

It confirms something we had been following: on one side, the bloc that Washington is considering formalizing around Demis Hassabis's proposal for a frontier model regulator modeled on the US financial supervisor; on the other, this Beijing-led club. Neither included Latin America in its original design, but both are courting it now that it is already built. At the closing, Huawei took the opportunity to present its Atlas 950 SuperPoD, a compute system that, according to the company, matches 6.7 times the capacity of Nvidia's NVL144 without using components of US origin, and Xi Jinping offered 5,000 AI training slots for developing countries. The underlying message is that infrastructure, and not just the rules, is also splitting in two.

## In the region

WAICO's operational launch redraws the map of alignments: Brazil, Cuba and Venezuela are already inside the China-led bloc, while the rest of Latin America watches without having been consulted by either of the two competing clubs. In parallel, Argentina is deepening the recentralization of its technology institutions: after the decree that removed the data and telecommunications agencies (AAIP and ENACOM) from the technical orbit of Science and Technology, [the departure of the area's secretary, Darío Genua, was confirmed](https://www.infobae.com/politica/2026/07/18/nueva-salida-en-el-gobierno-se-va-dario-genua-secretario-de-ciencia-y-tecnologia-y-diego-santilli-decide-su-reemplazo/), with no replacement formalized yet. And Panama announced that on July 30 it will launch its National AI Strategy, the sixth Central American country to formalize one.

## Threads we're following
