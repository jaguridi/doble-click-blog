## In 60 seconds

- **What happened.** AI was the risk most cited by 23 market contacts that the Federal Reserve Bank of New York surveyed between September and October.
- **Why it matters.** Big tech companies are piling up off-balance-sheet leases and guarantees that, according to the Fed, would make their exposure to a downturn larger than their liabilities.
- **What we don't know yet.** Whether the Fed's Board will include an AI-related stress assumption in the scenario for its 2027 stress test.

Among brokers, banks, funds and advisers, the concern was spread across several fronts. They mentioned elevated stock valuations and complex forms of financing. Also cyberattacks enabled by AI or harms caused by frontier models, the most capable of the moment. In the spring round, with 20 contacts, AI ranked third. That is what the [Financial Stability Report](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/files/financial-stability-report-20261113.pdf) records, published on November 13 by the Federal Reserve (Fed), the central bank of the United States. The survey reflects what those contacts perceive, not the Fed's own assessment.

The same document measures the financing of the boom. Debt issued for advanced compute infrastructure exceeded $680 billion as of October 2026, compared with $130 billion in all of 2023. The largest banking groups have about $1 trillion of exposure to that circuit. It includes big tech companies, data center developers, power companies, and special purpose and private credit vehicles. The latter are entities created to finance projects off the balance sheet of whoever promotes them.

Another layer does not show up in that debt. The report separately adds up leases that have not yet started, worth more than $1 trillion, and commitments through guarantees and contracts that exceed $2 trillion. Those commitments are left out of the liabilities that big tech companies report. That is why, the Fed warns, what they would lose in a crash could exceed what their accounts reflect. A bank or a fund that lends to them looking only at declared debt could be underestimating that risk. Each year the Fed reviews how much banks have at stake in this sector with its stress test.

## In the region

In Brazil, the Secretariat of Digital Government launched the web version of its self-assessment system for the ethical impact of AI, which previously worked only offline. It is available to the more than 250 agencies of SISP, which brings together the technology departments of the federal government, according to [Convergência Digital](https://convergenciadigital.com.br/governo/governo-adota-sistema-de-autoavaliacao-do-impacto-etico-em-inteligencia-artificial/). It was built with consulting from the World Bank and is being released as open source. The article does not say its use is mandatory.

In Belém, [Ponte Jornalismo](https://ponte.org/a-vizinhanca-da-inteligencia-artificial/) found no impact studies or licensing specific to the BEL1 data center, from Elea Data Centers and AXIA Energia. The project is minutes away from two occupations with hundreds of families, whose neighbors complain of a lack of information.
