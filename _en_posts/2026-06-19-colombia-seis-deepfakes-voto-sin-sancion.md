Two days before Colombia's presidential runoff, the campaign's sixth deepfake appeared: a fake video claiming that candidate Iván Cepeda has advanced colon cancer with metastasis and takes morphine daily. Cepeda responded by publishing a medical certificate that rules out any active recurrence and announced legal action ([El Tiempo](https://eltiempo.com/politica/elecciones-colombia-2026/ivan-cepeda-desmiente-rumores-sobre-problemas-de-salud)). It is the sixth fabrication generated with artificial intelligence in 21 days of campaigning, and this time the target is not the candidate's ideas, but his body.

We had been following this case as the most documented electoral AI experiment in the region, and today it comes to a close with an uncomfortable paradox. From 6 p.m. today until June 22 a public order law is in effect: the Technology Ministry (MinTIC) and the ColCERT response team are deploying real-time deepfake monitoring, hour by hour; the United States issued a formal alert about the process, and more than 50 civil society organizations asked both candidates to commit publicly to recognizing the result. But there is a timing problem that defines everything: Law 2502, the rule that provides the mechanism to sanction deepfakes, does not take effect until July 2026. The state can detect, it can warn and it can watch minute by minute —but it cannot punish.

That is the lesson Colombia leaves as a regional precedent, and it applies to everyone coming behind it. What has been shown is not just a record of six deepfakes in one campaign: it is that detection capacity without sanctioning capacity does not protect democracy but rather documents precisely how it erodes. Brazil, which votes in October 2026, Chile and the rest of the region reach their own electoral cycles with this experiment as the only precedent at hand. What happens on June 21 will be watched closely.

## In the region

Next week, on June 25 and 26, Santo Domingo hosts the [Third Ministerial Summit on the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence in Latin America and the Caribbean](https://foroialac.org/en/), organized by UNESCO, the development bank CAF and the Dominican digital government office. It brings together the region's science and technology ministers just four days after the Colombian vote, and in a charged context: a ban on Anthropic's most capable models decided without regional consultation, six electoral deepfakes in Colombia and the region's absence from the G7. Today Latin American developers can choose among three available frontier models —xAI's Grok 4, Zhipu's GLM-5.2 and OpenAI's GPT-5.5—, but none is based in the region, has governance obligations toward it or went through a regional consultation. Whether the summit produces concrete commitments or just another declaration of principles will say a lot about the region's real capacity to coordinate.
