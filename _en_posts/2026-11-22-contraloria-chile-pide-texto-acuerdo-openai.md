## In 60 seconds

- **What happened.** Chile's Office of the Comptroller General of the Republic gave the Ministry of Science 10 business days to report on the "OpenAI for Chile" memorandum.
- **Why it matters.** The ministry will have to clarify in writing whether that agreement with a private company is subject to the state's public procurement rules.
- **What we don't know yet.** How the ministry will respond, when the deadline expires, since it runs from the letter's full processing, and whether a ruling will follow.

Minister Ximena Lincolao and Sam Altman signed the memorandum on November 16 in San Francisco. The Comptroller's official letter, dated the 18th and made public on the 20th, asks for its full text, its duration, the state's obligations and whether there are financial commitments, according to [BioBioChile](https://www.biobiochile.cl/noticias/nacional/chile/2026/11/20/acuerdo-con-openai-bajo-la-lupa-contraloria-da-10-dias-a-ministra-lincolao-para-explicarlo.shtml). It adds the expenses of the minister's trip and the memorandum with ElevenLabs. It responds to a request from Deputy Jaime Araya and, for now, only gathers information: it does not rule on legality.

The request for the full text contrasts with the ministry's initial stance. After the signing, the ministry said the agreement would be free of charge and non-exclusive, and that it would not publish the document, as reported [on November 18](https://dobleclick.jaguridi.cl/en/2026/11/18/nvidia-mas-que-duplica-ventas-trimestre.html). Now it will have to hand it over to the oversight body, which has gone from receiving complaints from lawmakers to requesting information with a deadline.

The question about public procurement could have the greatest practical reach. If those rules apply, any later use of OpenAI tools in state services might have to go through them. The memorandum alone would not be enough. The letter also asks how personal data are protected, including international transfers. And it requests similar agreements signed with companies since March 11, 2026, so the Comptroller would receive an inventory of those agreements with names and dates.

## In the region

In Brazil, the National Data Protection Authority (ANPD) presumes that systems that generate natural-language content are likely to be accessed by minors. That is what its [guide on Law 15.211](https://www.infobae.com/tecno/2026/11/21/brasil-incluyo-a-chatbots-de-ia-juegos-y-tiendas-de-apps-entre-los-servicios-sujetos-a-su-ley-infantil/) (via Infobae), known as ECA Digital, establishes. The presumption can only be set aside by justifying the specific case. Chatbots are among eight categories, along with games, app stores and operating systems. The guide could not be found on the ANPD website as of November 22.

In Peru, President Keiko Fujimori told the CGTN network, at the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation forum, that she will apply AI in health, education and government procedures. She did not specify a responsible agency, budget or timeline, according to [Infobae](https://www.infobae.com/peru/2026/11/22/keiko-fujimori-desea-aplicar-inteligencia-artificial-en-el-estado-queremos-darle-mucha-mas-velocidad-y-transparencia/).
