## In 60 seconds

- **What happened.** Levent Alpöge, a mathematician at Anthropic, claims to have proved, with Claude, the Riemann hypothesis, posed in 1859, and its generalized version.
- **Why it matters.** Without a readable text, accepting the result depends for now on a few Lean specialists and on automated checks, not on a journal.
- **What we don't know yet.** When a version that mathematicians can read will appear, and whether a specialized journal will put it through peer review.

Andrew Sutherland, of the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT), compiled the proof and ran it through the Lean kernel using only the three standard axioms. The kernel is the part of the program that accepts or rejects each step of a proof. [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-ai-riemann-hypothesis-proof-is-almost-certainly-true-independent-mathematicians-attest/) reports it. In the same article, Kevin Buzzard, of Imperial College London, said he is still waiting for a successful run of the comparator. That check rules out shortcuts that change the meaning of the statement: a correct proof of a different claim would not solve the problem.

The Riemann hypothesis is one of the Millennium Prize Problems. Alpöge published his [text](https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf) on November 20. In it, he says the proof is formalized in Lean, a language in which a computer checks each step, and that for now it exists only in that form. According to the same document, he spoke with Dario Amodei before making it public. That the author works at the company that created the model used suggests that review by academics at other institutions should carry more weight than usual.

In practice, judgment on the result would rest with those who know how to audit that code. For journals, that could reverse the usual order: the technical verdict would arrive before the paper. The same day, Ravi Vakil shared on his [blog](https://math216.wordpress.com/2026/11/20/hurried-announcement-alessandro-masullo-grh/) a human proof of the generalized version, by Alessandro Masullo. Hours later, he reported that problems had been pointed out to him. In October, OpenAI had published [722 manuscripts from an internal model](https://dobleclick.jaguridi.cl/en/2026/10/07/722-manuscritos-modelo-openai-162-prueba-formal.html), of which 162 came with a formal proof.
