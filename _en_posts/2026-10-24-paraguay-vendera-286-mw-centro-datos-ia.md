## In 60 seconds

- **What happened.** Paraguay's state-owned power company signed a 15-year contract on October 22 to supply an AI data center in Yguazú.
- **Why it matters.** The contract has a ceiling of 286 megawatts, and anything the complex consumes above that would fall outside the state rate.
- **What we don't know yet.** What role Crusoe, the US company partnered with Araico, plays in the project, something the head of the state company did not specify.

Supply begins on December 1, 2026, with 6 megawatts (MW). It rises to 146 MW from April 1, 2028, and reaches 286 MW in April 2029. That is how Miguel Báez, head of the National Electricity Administration (ANDE), laid it out, according to [ABC Color](https://www.abc.com.py/economia/2026/10/23/es-beneficioso-para-la-ande-centro-de-ia-pagara-entre-us-4891-y-5639-por-mwh-de-la-energia/). The customer is Kuntur, a Paraguayan company linked to Araico.

The initial rate is $48.91 per megawatt hour (MWh) if the power is received at 220 kilovolts, and $56.39 if it is received at 23. In other words, connecting at high voltage is cheaper for the company. The price is adjusted each year for US inflation, with a floor of 2.5%. The connection works are paid for by Kuntur. All of these terms come from the state company, through that news report.

ANDE set the 286 MW limit. Báez said that if the complex requires more power, Kuntur will have to obtain it from another generator. That surplus would fall outside the contract and outside the price the state company charges. This suggests that ANDE's power over the cost of the project's energy extends only up to that tier. When asked, Báez also did not specify Crusoe's role. That US company is Araico's partner in a compute complex powered by Itaipú, according to [DPL News](https://dplnews.com/centro-de-datos-crusoe-araico-itaipu-paraguay/). That partnership was reported [on October 22](https://dobleclick.jaguridi.cl/en/2026/10/22/php-corrige-dos-fallas-criticas-servidores-web.html).
