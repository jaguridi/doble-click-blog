## In 60 seconds

- **What happened.** Colombia's government and Google presented an AI training partnership on October 13, with scholarships, school licenses and courses.
- **Why it matters.** Public schools and state AI training would be tied for four years to a single provider's tools, under conditions that are still unknown.
- **What we don't know yet.** How much the state pays, what rules protect students' data, and whether the list notified starting October 26 reaches Chocó.

The 15,000 scholarships are for Google Career Certificates, which are taken on Coursera with up to one year of access, according to [the ICT Ministry](https://www.mintic.gov.co/portal/inicio/Sala-de-prensa/Noticias/441270:Gobierno-nacional-y-Google-se-unen-para-transformar-la-educacion-en-Colombia-con-nuevas-rutas-formativas-en-inteligencia-artificial). They are awarded by the NGO Colnodo. Priority goes to Chocó, Valle del Cauca, Risaralda, Caldas and Quindío, the departments affected by the August earthquake. The agreement is part of Talento ColombIA, the strategy led by Vice President José Manuel Restrepo.

The largest piece goes to classrooms. Google will provide up to 7 million active licenses for Google Workspace for Education, its suite of tools for schools, to public schools over four years. The first phase will be in Chocó and Buenaventura. On top of that comes training through SENA, the public technical apprenticeship service, with a potential reach of one million people. The license figure is a ceiling, not a confirmed number of users.

The scholarships certify in Google products, and the licenses install that same ecosystem in state schools. For a school in Buenaventura that adopts the suite, what happens to its students' data will depend on terms the official statement does not include. The same goes for the service when the four years are up: continuing to use it could mean a new cost for the education budget, or a migration. Until those conditions are published, the size of the announcement says little about its real cost to the state.

## In the region

In Brazil, Justice Marco Aurélio Bellizze of the Superior Electoral Court (TSE) ordered Jorge Messias to delete a pro-Lula video from his Instagram. Messias is the Solicitor General of the Union and has 24 hours. Bellizze partially upheld a complaint by Flávio Bolsonaro because, in his view, the video did not disclose that its characters were made with AI, according to [Correio Braziliense](https://www.correiobraziliense.com.br/politica/2026/10/7520017-ministro-do-tse-manda-jorge-messias-retirar-video-pro-lula-feito-com-ia.html). He wrote that the rule does not make removal conditional on how realistic the content is. It is a preliminary decision: the fine and whether to classify it as a deepfake are left for the final ruling. On October 8, the court had ordered Flávio Bolsonaro to take down another AI video without a disclosure, a case covered [on October 11](https://dobleclick.jaguridi.cl/en/2026/10/11/stargate-argentina-un-ano-openai-posible-cliente.html).
