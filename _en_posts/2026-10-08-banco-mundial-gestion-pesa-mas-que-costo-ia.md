## In 60 seconds

- **What happened.** The World Bank published its semiannual report on Latin America and the Caribbean, devoted to AI, and projects growth of 2.2% for the region in 2026.
- **Why it matters.** If the bottleneck lies in how companies are managed, making AI cheaper to access might not be enough to raise their productivity.
- **What we don't know yet.** Whether labor and education ministries will use the bank's division between jobs AI can enhance and jobs exposed to it to decide what training they offer.

About 8% of the region's workforce holds skilled jobs that AI could enhance, according to [the World Bank's press release](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2026/10/06/latin-america-caribbean-economic-update). Around 10% work in routine cognitive occupations, the most exposed to automation. About a quarter hold routine manual jobs that, according to the bank, a second wave could reach. The report, [Harnessing the AI Transition](https://www.worldbank.org/en/region/lac/publication/perspectivas-economicas-america-latina-caribe), compares its 2026 projection with 2.4% in 2025.

On the individual side, a median of 17% of working-age adults say they use generative AI tools. It is a figure for self-reported, individual use. Among companies, the bank sees broad adoption that rarely reaches their core processes.

According to the bank, the main barriers are management capacity, skills and the reorganization of companies, not cost or access. For an economy ministry or a business chamber, that suggests a policy focused on cheaper licenses or connectivity would target a secondary bottleneck. A company can have the tool installed and keep working the same way. In that case, the share of users would grow without any change in what the company produces per hour worked.

## In the region

In Costa Rica, the University of Costa Rica's PROLEDI program and the Observatorio Complutense documented 257 disinformation incidents in the election campaign, between January and March. AI was involved in one in five, and 77.4% had no visible correction or measure, according to [Semanario Universidad](https://semanariouniversidad.com/pais/informe-ucr-inteligencia-artificial-potencio-la-desinformacion-durante-la-campana-electoral-de-2026/) (via Semanario Universidad).

In Brazil, Gecex Resolution 971 makes it cheaper to import AI units for video analysis in vehicles until September 30, 2028. It does not cover servers or the GPU processors that data centers use to run models, according to [Convergência Digital](https://convergenciadigital.com.br/governo/governo-zera-imposto-de-importacao-de-equipamentos-de-inteligencia-artificial-mas-nao-altera-o-redata/) (via Convergência Digital).
