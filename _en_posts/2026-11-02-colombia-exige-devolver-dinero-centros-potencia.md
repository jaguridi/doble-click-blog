## In 60 seconds

- **What happened.** On October 30, Colombia's Ministry of Information and Communications Technologies (MinTIC) demanded the return of the 234.399 billion pesos earmarked for the PotencIA AI Centers.
- **Why it matters.** The money is held by a trust company, so the demand will only take effect if the ministry defines how to collect it and from whom.
- **What we don't know yet.** Whether the Comptroller's Office will open a fiscal liability proceeding based on what it observed about the project's funds.

The Office of the Comptroller General of the Republic measured construction progress of 2.09% at the PotencIA Centers, with a complete standstill since November 2025. This is reported in [the MinTIC statement](https://www.mintic.gov.co/portal/inicio/Sala-de-prensa/Noticias/442339:Los-Centros-PotencIA-una-promesa-de-234-000-millones-de-pesos-que-nunca-se-hizo-realidad), which also cites failures in planning, in clearing land sites for the project, and in construction permits. The oversight body has been monitoring the project since January.

The project dates from the previous government. It promised 75 AI learning centers in 26 departments, and later the figure discussed was 60 centers. The contract with Findeter was signed in September 2024, and 100% of the funds were transferred to a trust company, an entity that manages funds on behalf of others. According to the ministry, no center is operating. Minister Alexandra Falla Zerrate said she was acting on the instructions of President Abelardo De La Espriella. The assessment comes from the current government, which is judging a project of its predecessor, and from the Comptroller's figures that it cites.

The statement does not specify the collection mechanism or to whom the order is addressed. Without that procedure, the order does not say who must move the funds or by when. That suggests its real effect can only be measured once the ministry publishes how it will collect.

## In the region

In Brazil, the Lula government wants the Chamber of Deputies to approve the legal framework for AI before the end of the year. It is bill (PL) 2.338/2023, already approved by the Senate. O Globo reports this, as picked up by [Times Brasil](https://timesbrasil.com.br/brasil/governo-quer-aprovar-regras-para-ia-e-big-techs-ainda-em-2026/) (via Times Brasil). The framework provides for fines of up to 50 million reais. The plan adds PL 4.675/2025, on digital competition, which gives Cade, the competition authority, ex ante rules for large platforms. Its rapporteur, Aliel Machado, plans to discuss the text with party leaders this week. The government expects the newly elected Congress to be less favorable. It is a goal, not a scheduled vote.
