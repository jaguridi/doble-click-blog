The block on Anthropic's Fable 5 and Mythos 5 models changed in nature today: it is no longer just a matter of export controls, but a national security issue with a political way out in sight. The US National Cyber Director, Sean Cairncross —part of the National Security Council, not of the commerce office that imposed the restriction—, formally joined the negotiations, and the newspaper *The Globe and Mail* reported this morning that there is a "deal in the works" with a possible resolution before June 30 ([status of the block](https://anthropic.com/news/fable-mythos-access) · [report on the deal](https://explainx.ai/blog/is-fable-5-back-2026)). As a reminder: for eleven days these advanced models have been banned for part of the world, including Latin America, in an episode we have been following closely.

That an official from the National Security Council —and not from the Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS), which applied the ban— is sitting at the table raises the case to the highest political level and suggests that Washington is setting the standard for who can access frontier AI models, systems capable of tasks so sophisticated that they are considered sensitive for defense. For the region there is an uncomfortable detail: if restoring access requires citizenship verification or biometric data —something hinted at by Anthropic's June 19 privacy policy update—, Latin American users could be left out of a solution designed for US residents. Yesterday, in addition, a commercial change took effect without notice: Fable 5 stopped being included in the subscription and began to be charged through additional credits.
