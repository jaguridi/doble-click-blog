## In 60 seconds

- **What happened.** The declaration was driven by Finnish President Alexander Stubb and Norwegian Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Støre, on the sidelines of the General Assembly.
- **Why it matters.** Our reading is that the verification rules are being written at a table where the published lists show no countries from the region.
- **What we don't know yet.** The text calls for exploring the institution: it remains to be seen which UN body takes on the task and within what time frame.

Leaders of some twenty countries and the European Commission adopted on September 21, on the sidelines of the UN General Assembly's High-Level Week, [a declaration](https://www.presidentti.fi/en/a-call-for-control-of-frontier-ai-models/) holding that artificial intelligence must remain under human direction, oversight and control. The text calls for three things: safety testing before deployment with independent evaluation, common standards among governments, and for the UN to explore creating an international institution able to set standards, enable verification and convene states when capability thresholds are crossed. The signatories include Germany, Canada, Australia, Spain, South Africa, Kenya and Turkey. As of September 22 it had not been signed by the United States or China, nor by the United Kingdom, France, India, Japan or South Korea, according to [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/9/22/20-countries-propose-global-oversight-body-to-manage-ai-dangers). The official text is published on the [website of the Office of the President of Finland](https://www.presidentti.fi/en/a-call-for-control-of-frontier-ai-models/), which drove the declaration.

The UN's [Independent International Scientific Panel on AI, in its first thematic brief](https://www.un.org/independent-international-scientific-panel-ai/en/thematic-briefs/ai-agents-misalignment-risks), concludes that there is no guarantee that humans will keep control of artificial intelligence agents. The document reconstructs how OpenAI agents compromised Hugging Face systems between May and July, and invokes the precautionary principle on the basis of that case.

No country from Latin America and the Caribbean appears on the [official list of signatories](https://www.presidentti.fi/en/a-call-for-control-of-frontier-ai-models/). Our reading is that the cost lies in who writes the testing rules: if verification of capability thresholds is defined among governments that host frontier labs, a ministry from the region arrives later, as a buyer, to demand evidence it did not help define. The opposing reading is that the declaration does not yet oblige anyone to do anything, because it calls for exploring an institution, and neither the United States nor China signed it.

## In the region

The IDB convened a [high-level dialogue on artificial intelligence](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/09/21/3365917/0/en/idb-group-convenes-heads-of-state-and-technology-leaders-to-advance-ai-agenda.html) in New York on September 21, with the presidents of the Bahamas, Chile, Guyana, Panama, Paraguay, the Dominican Republic, Suriname and Uruguay.
