## In 60 seconds

- **What happened.** Agência Lupa confirmed 1,423 pieces of political content made or altered with AI in the Brazilian campaign, in a report published before the runoff.
- **Why it matters.** The pieces published by the candidates themselves have a named party responsible, and that could make it easier for the Electoral Justice system to review them one by one.
- **What we don't know yet.** Whether the Electoral Public Prosecutor's Office or the courts will use Lupa's database, and what the platforms will do with the unlabeled pieces.

Of the confirmed total, 768 pieces did not disclose that they had been made with AI, according to [the VigIA report](https://uploads.agencialupa.org/2026/10/VigIA-Relatorio-3o-parcial.pdf). Among the labeled ones, 352 carried the author's notice and 300 the platform's. There were 520 deepfakes (videos or images that make a real person say or do something) and another 196 pieces that combined them with synthetic content. Instagram accounted for 794 cases and Facebook for 690, and the same piece of content can appear on both. Lula appears in 508 pieces and Flávio Bolsonaro in 247.

VigIA is the project Lupa uses to monitor AI-made political content in these elections. During the period it collected 2,542 suspicious pieces of content and analyzed 1,486, with an average of 20.9 confirmed per day. The pieces come in because readers send them or because the team finds them. That is why the number reflects what was detected, not what share of campaign advertising complies with disclosure. The most common purpose was promoting candidacies (786 cases), while 360 pieces attacked a rival. Of the deepfakes, 123 showed candidates performing physical or work feats. One example is the videos in which Júlio Cesar, who won Piauí's Senate seat, does sumo and rides a skateboard.

Candidates' official accounts published 548 of the confirmed pieces. Lupa makes its records available to the Electoral Justice system and to the platforms. In those cases, the author is an identifiable candidacy. That suggests this group could be the most direct starting point for a review by the Electoral Public Prosecutor's Office.
