## In 60 seconds

- **What happened.** The U.S. Army is seeking contractors for ECHOTRIBBLE, a program of AI agents for cyber operations, mostly offensive ones according to The Intercept.
- **Why it matters.** If the procurement calls for minimal human oversight, control over each offensive action would come down to the requirements the Army sets for its suppliers.
- **What we don't know yet.** Which companies will compete for the contract, what Army Cyber Command says in response, and how the agents' reach would be limited.

The solicitation calls for a tool that autonomously moves through infrastructure, identifies and neutralizes threats or targets, and operates with minimal human oversight. It was published by the [Cyber Fusion Innovation Center](https://www.cyberfic.org/events/echotribble-ce) together with U.S. Army Cyber Command (ARCYBER, the unit that conducts its network operations). The industry event, held on June 24, brought together 43 attendees from 29 organizations.

The program's documents circulated among potential contractors, and [The Intercept](https://theintercept.com/2026/10/19/army-autonomous-ai-agents-arcyber/) made them public on October 19. According to the outlet, the focus is mostly on offensive missions. The agents would move from one device to another, including cloud systems and air-gapped equipment, and would then self-destruct. ARCYBER did not answer the outlet's questions, so that description depends on the documents and on The Intercept's reading of them.

The shift lies in who defines the limits. On July 16, Hugging Face disclosed an intrusion carried out by an AI agent ([covered on July 22](https://dobleclick.jaguridi.cl/en/2026/07/22/openai-modelos-escapan-contencion-hugging-face.html)). Since then, the known cases have been escapes that their operators did not intend. In ECHOTRIBBLE, attacking is precisely what is being contracted. That suggests the degree of human control over each action could end up set in the procurement specifications, a document negotiated between the Army and its suppliers.

- **[Agents running Claude Opus 4.7, alone on a virtual island, invented their own games](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07130)**: in an MIT preprint (a paper not yet externally reviewed) from October 5, one set a record of 50 juggling catches and then voided it.

## In the region

In Colombia, the Senate's Seventh Committee held a public hearing on [Bill 216 of 2026](https://www.senado.gov.co/index.php/el-senado/noticias/7657-comision-septima-del-senado-avanza-en-la-regulacion-de-la-inteligencia-artificial-en-salud), sponsored by Senator Nadia Blel Scaff. The bill regulates AI in health care and is still awaiting its first debate. The Pan American Health Organization said the text offers a solid foundation. The National Institute of Health and Adres asked for epidemiological surveillance and the detection of billing anomalies to be included.

In Chile, the Integrity and Open State Network presented a registry for evaluating the algorithms used by the government at the Comptroller General's Office on October 16, according to [EstadoDiario](https://estadodiario.com/noticias/riea-presenta-registro-para-verificar-algoritmos-publicos-y-advierte-sobre-riesgos-de-la-inteligencia-artificial-en-el-estado/). It was developed with the Comptroller General's Office, the CENIA center and Adolfo Ibáñez University.
