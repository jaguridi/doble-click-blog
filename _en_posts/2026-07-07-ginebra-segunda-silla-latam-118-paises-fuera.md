The picture of global artificial intelligence governance shifted a little in the region's favor today: it was confirmed that Costa Rica co-facilitated, together with Spain, the preparatory process for the [first UN Global Dialogue on AI Governance](https://www.un.org/sg/en/content/sg/statements/2026-07-06/secretary-generals-remarks-the-opening-of-the-first-global-dialogue-artificial-intelligence-governance-delivered), which opened this week in Geneva. It is a second Latin American institutional leadership role, adding to that of El Salvador, whose ambassador Egriselda López co-chairs the dialogue together with Estonia. Yesterday we reported that the region arrived with "one seat"; today there are, properly speaking, two.

The caveat comes on the same day, and it is a harsh one. The Independent International Scientific Panel advising the process, co-chaired by Yoshua Bengio and Nobel Peace Prize laureate Maria Ressa, put a figure on the table: 118 countries, most of them from the Global South, are effectively left out of the substantive AI governance discussions. Two leadership seats do not cancel out that underlying asymmetry, and no Latin American government confirmed a ministerial delegation of its own, unlike India. At the opening, Secretary-General António Guterres called for banning lethal autonomous weapons and warned against letting AI "vibe-code" humanity's future. Meanwhile, Sam Altman pressed his idea of creating an AI safety body modeled on the International Atomic Energy Agency, a proposal at odds with the multilateral process that the UN, UNESCO and the International Telecommunication Union are pushing this very week.

## In the region

The operational side of the issue showed up in three pieces today. First, the driving force behind Latam-GPT explained [why AI models make more mistakes when they talk about Latin America](https://www.lanacion.com.ar/economia/IA/inteligencia-artificial-el-impulsor-de-latam-gpt-explico-por-que-los-modelos-de-ia-se-equivocan-mas-nid06072026/): between 20% and 40% more "hallucinations" (made-up answers that sound plausible) because of the scarcity of regional data in training, a concrete argument for data sovereignty. Second, AWS warned that [90% of AI projects in the region do not get past the proof of concept](https://www.infobae.com/tecno/2026/07/06/el-reto-de-la-ia-en-america-latina-por-que-el-90-de-los-proyectos-no-superan-la-prueba-de-concepto/), and attributes the failure to organizational execution more than to technology. And third, a sign that the region is already buying and deploying: Spain's [Substrate AI won a contract with the Buenos Aires City Government](https://forbes.es/ultima-hora/972769/substrate-ai-se-adjudica-un-contrato-en-buenos-aires-para-desplegar-su-plataforma-de-ia-en-el-gobierno/) for its platform to be the foundation of BAX, the Buenos Aires city government's AI assistant.
