## In 60 seconds

- **What happened.** Brazil's Ministry of Justice proposed that the Federal Prosecutor's Office for Citizens' Rights sue Meta over a lack of transparency in its moderation.
- **Why it matters.** If a court accepts the measure, Meta could not suspend media outlets or civil society organizations without a person first reviewing the case.
- **What we don't know yet.** Whether the Prosecutor's Office will file the lawsuit before the October 25 presidential runoff, and how Meta will respond.

The proposed injunction would give Meta 15 days to restore what it restricted without a specific justification, with a daily penalty of 500,000 reais. That is according to [O Globo](https://oglobo.globo.com/politica/eleicoes-2026/noticia/2026/10/17/ministerio-da-justica-aciona-mpf-no-caso-meta-falta-transparencia.ghtml), which obtained the two technical notes signed on October 16. They were signed by the National Secretariat for Digital Rights. According to those documents, Meta does not disclose its criteria for removing posts, suspending accounts and restricting reach. As of October 18, the notes had not been published on the ministry's website, so their content is known only through that news report.

The request goes beyond restoring content. The notes propose banning suspensions and reach restrictions without prior human review when they affect media outlets, civil society organizations and student groups. The proposal is addressed to the Prosecutor's Office, which is part of the Federal Public Prosecutor's Office. In addition, the ministry asks the National Data Protection Authority (ANPD) to consider an inspection and an audit of Meta's automated systems. That agency oversees how personal information is handled in Brazil.

To comply with that rule, Meta would have to know what kind of holder is behind each account before acting on it. If a judge upholds it, that classification criterion could come to depend on a court decision and not only on the company. The ANPD route runs on a different timeline: an inspection of the automated systems would not end with the vote.

## In the region

In Brazil, the 1st Federal Court of Porto Alegre ordered Meta to restore, within two days, the accounts of four student groups. They belong to UFRGS and UFCSPA, two federal universities. The accounts were suspended on October 6, and the lawsuit was brought by the Federal Public Prosecutor's Office, according to [Agência Brasil](https://agenciabrasil.ebc.com.br/justica/noticia/2026-10/justica-federal-manda-meta-restabelecer-contas-estudantis-do-rs). The report does not give the date of the decision or say whether the suspension was automated.
