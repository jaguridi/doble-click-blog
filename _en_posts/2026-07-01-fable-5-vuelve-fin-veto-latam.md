As of this July 1, [Claude Fable 5 is available again worldwide](https://www.anthropic.com/news/fable-mythos-access). On June 30, the US Department of Commerce lifted the export controls that had blocked public access to the model since June 12, closing 19 days of an unprecedented episode: for the first time, a frontier artificial intelligence model, among the most capable in existence, was suspended for the general public by decision of a government.

The outcome was not just a matter of "available again." Along with the reopening, Anthropic announced a new cooperation framework with the US government: early access to its models before public launch and a joint scheme with Amazon, Microsoft and Google to rate the danger of *jailbreaks* (techniques that try to get around a model's safety restrictions). Mythos 5, the more powerful sibling model, remains restricted to approved US critical infrastructure organizations. In other words, access was restored, but a channel through which Washington exerts influence before a model reaches the public was also institutionalized.

For Latin America, the episode leaves an uncomfortable lesson. The region uses these tools every day (developers, companies, students) and had no voice either when the model was switched off or when it was switched back on. Everything was resolved through market pressure, reputation and direct negotiation between a company and a government in the North. Had the affected population been Latin American, it is not clear what leverage would have existed to speed up an equivalent response.

## In the region

Panama joined [Pax Silica](https://www.state.gov/pax-silica), the US State Department alliance that links AI cooperation to commitments on critical minerals and supply chain traceability. It is the fifth Latin American country to sign, after Argentina, Chile, Costa Rica and El Salvador. The pattern is clear and is advancing country by country, but there is still no coordinated regional position (from CELAC, ECLAC or CAF) on a scheme that ties access to technology to conditions on natural resources.

## Threads we're following

This close adds to the story we have been following since mid-June, when the United States unexpectedly suspended access to Fable 5 on national security grounds and opened a negotiation whose terms were never fully public. That ban had "cracked open" at the end of the month with partial reopenings; today the chapter closes with global access restored and a new framework of prior cooperation between Anthropic and the US government. What remains to be seen is whether that framework (government early access to models) becomes the norm for the whole industry.
