## In 60 seconds

- **What happened.** The energy Paraguay sells to Brazil from Itaipú fell from 22.2 million megawatt hours in 2023 to 10.5 million in 2025.
- **Why it matters.** Every megawatt hour Paraguay consumes at home is no longer sold to Brazil, and with it the payment Paraguay receives for ceding it goes down.
- **What we don't know yet.** What rate Brazil and Paraguay will set for 2027 in the review of Annex C of the Itaipú Treaty, which has yet to conclude.

Between January and September 2026, payments to Paraguay for ceding Itaipú energy fell by $33 million. The figure was published by [JOTA](https://www.jota.info/tributos-e-empresas/mercado/ia-entra-na-conta-da-negociacao-de-itaipu) on October 31, with data from Brazil's Ministry of Mines and Energy. Between 2023 and 2025, the dam's supply to Paraguay rose from 19.5 to 25.8 million megawatt hours (MWh). That is the unit used to bill the energy consumed. The figures come through that news report, without the ministry's report linked.

JOTA links that growing consumption to the review of Annex C, the part of the treaty that sets the dam's financial terms. In May 2024, both governments kept the rate at $19.28 per kilowatt (kW) per month until December 2026. For 2027, sources in the Brazilian government are weighing a rate of around $15, according to the outlet. JOTA adds to that calculation an AI compute complex that Crusoe and Araico plan to operate from mid-2027, next to the Itaipú substation.

The decision on that consumption goes through the National Electricity Administration (ANDE), Paraguay's state-owned utility. It has already committed to deliver up to 286 megawatts to Kuntur's compute complex in Yguazú, as reported [on October 24](https://dobleclick.jaguridi.cl/en/2026/10/24/paraguay-vendera-286-mw-centro-datos-ia.html). That suggests a trade-off: each ANDE contract with a large local consumer could reduce the energy Brazil receives through cession and what Paraguay collects as compensation.

## In the region

In Brazil, appellate judge (desembargador) Marçal Henri dos Santos Figueiredo sits on the Regional Labor Court of the 4th Region. He warned that his panel will not hear abusive motions for clarification drafted with AI. It may sanction them as bad-faith litigation, according to [Migalhas](https://www.migalhas.com.br/quentes/467655/desembargador-adverte-advogados-por-uso-de-ia-em-embargo-oab-rs-reage). He said the average number of such motions in his chambers rose from about 30 to 70. It was a warning, with no fine reported and no case number. Leonardo Lamachia, president of the Rio Grande do Sul section of the Brazilian Bar Association (OAB/RS), called it "quase que uma intimidação".
