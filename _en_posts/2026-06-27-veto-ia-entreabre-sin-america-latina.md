The US ban on the most advanced artificial intelligence models opened a crack for the first time, but only for a closed group. US Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick sent a formal letter on June 26 authorizing Anthropic to give access to its Mythos 5 model to more than a hundred organizations classified as "trusted partners" (Fortune 500 companies and designated federal agencies), while the other blocked model, Fable 5, remains unavailable to the general public ([aitoolly](https://aitoolly.com/ai-news/article/2026-06-27-us-government-grants-anthropic-permission-to-release-mythos-model-to-selected-trusted-partners) · [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/tech/anthropic-mythos-trusted-partners)). It is the first partial resolution of a blockade that has now lasted fifteen days: early this month Washington banned two Anthropic frontier models over a safety risk, and since then the region has not been part of the conversation.

What matters is not only that access is coming back, but *how* it comes back. The restoration is "America first": the more than one hundred authorized organizations are large US companies and federal agencies, and there is no Latin American organization in this first round. The region did not take part in the prior negotiations, was not consulted, and has no mechanism to influence who gets onto that list of "trusted partners." It is also the first time the US government has explicitly put into practice a registry of organizations approved to use frontier models, the most capable AI systems on the market. If that mechanism becomes permanent, the question for Latin America is no longer when access returns but whether there is any institutional path onto the list. Today there is none.

The timing underscores the gap. The arrangement is formalized just one day after the region's technology ministers adopted, in Santo Domingo, the Declaration of the UNESCO summit on AI ethics, their first attempt to set a common position. While the region produces its governance roadmap, Washington is rolling out a tiered system of access to the most capable models that, for now, does not include it. Access to OpenAI's GPT-5.6 also remains under a preventive restriction: two of the most advanced systems in the world remain wholly or partly out of the region's reach.

## In the region

An investigation by The Intercept documented that the Pentagon, through its Special Operations Command South, runs a platform called "La Tilde": a Spanish-language news site aimed at Latin American readers and produced with generative AI, with no identifiable authors and with the only notice of public funding buried in the site's information page ([The Intercept](https://theintercept.com/2026/06/02/la-tilde-propaganda-latin-america-pentagon/)). Atlantic Council researchers describe it as "artificial intelligence from start to finish," and the site reportedly plans versions for Ecuador, El Salvador, Guyana, Honduras, Jamaica, Panama and Peru. The contrast is hard to ignore: the same administration that blocked a model because of its danger to critical infrastructure uses generative AI at scale to produce messages aimed at the region. Organizations such as the EFF and Privacy International warn that the notice complies with the law but does not guarantee transparency, because the cost of producing geotargeted propaganda with these models is approaching zero.
