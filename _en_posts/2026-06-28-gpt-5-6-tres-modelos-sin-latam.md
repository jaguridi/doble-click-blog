What was a leak last week is today a product with a name and a price. OpenAI unveiled **GPT-5.6**, split into three models (Sol, Terra and Luna), and confirmed what had been anticipated: it went straight to an access list of about twenty organizations approved by the White House, with no availability for the general public or for Latin America ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/previewing-gpt-5-6-sol/)). This completes a pattern that began two days ago: on June 26, the most capable models of the two most influential frontier labs, OpenAI's GPT-5.6 and Anthropic's Mythos 5, were placed on the same day under access lists approved by the US government.

The detail that did not exist before is pricing, and that is where the regional angle appears. Sol is the flagship model ($5/$30 per million input/output tokens) and sets a new record on a standard benchmark of programming tasks; Terra comes in at half the price of the previous generation; and Luna, at $1/$6 per million, falls in a range competitive with regional models and with several open-source alternatives. When access is expanded, Luna will probably be Latin American developers' gateway to this family. The problem is the "when": OpenAI only says "in the coming weeks," with no date, no criteria and no mechanism for consulting the region. The difference from the Fable 5 ban, which was reactive and followed a safety failure, is that here the control is preventive: the list of who gets in was defined *before* the launch.

Meanwhile, the map of what can actually be used in the region is rearranging itself. This week, independent evaluations documented that **GLM-5.2**, an open-weights model from China's Zhipu AI (under an MIT license), matched Mythos 5 on safety tests, the first open model to reach that threshold, and it is available without any government list ([explainX](https://explainx.ai/blog/is-fable-5-back-2026)). This is not a deliberate choice by the region's developers but the result of decisions made in Washington: the open model filling the safety gap is Chinese, and the high-end option accessible without an approved company ended up being Grok.

## In the region

June 26 produced two divergent responses to the same problem. That day, Latin America's technology ministers adopted, in Santo Domingo, the Declaration of the UNESCO summit on AI ethics, their first attempt to set a common position. A few hours later, OpenAI launched its most capable model under a list controlled by the White House. The Declaration contains no mechanism that has a say in who gets onto those lists, and its roadmap has a twelve-month horizon. The concrete result, today, is that the model with safety capabilities equivalent to Mythos 5 and available without restrictions in the region is open-weights and of Chinese origin. Access is not being decided at the table where the region agreed on its principles.
