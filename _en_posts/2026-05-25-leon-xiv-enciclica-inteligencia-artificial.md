Pope Leo XIV today published *Magnifica Humanitas*, [the first papal encyclical devoted entirely to artificial intelligence](https://www.vaticannews.va/en/pope/news/2026-05/pope-leo-xiv-first-encyclical-magnifica-humanitas.html). The document, 235 pages long and in five chapters, was signed on May 15 (the 135th anniversary of *Rerum Novarum*) and puts forward a central idea: technology "is not neutral; it takes on the face of those who conceive it, finance it, regulate it and use it." It also warns that control of AI must not remain "in the hands of a few."

What makes this text especially relevant is its reach. With about 1.4 billion Catholics worldwide and some 500 million in Latin America, it is the institutional statement on AI ethics with the largest potential audience in history: more than any multilateral body. A detail worth noting is who joined the presentation: [Leo XIV presented the document alongside Christopher Olah](https://www.ncronline.org/vatican/vatican-news/pope-leo-present-his-encyclical-ai-alongside-anthropic-co-founder), co-founder of Anthropic and head of that company's interpretability research. For the region, the question is concrete and has historical roots: if *Rerum Novarum* (1891) shaped Latin American trade unionism and social policy for more than a century, which institutions (parties, Catholic universities such as the PUC, the Javeriana or the PUCP) could channel the impact of *Magnifica Humanitas* into AI governance?

## In the region

The week concentrates an unusual density of governance decisions. Today, the Catholic Church formally enters the global debate with the statement with the widest population reach in the field. On Wednesday the 27th, Brazil decides whether or not it will have the region's first AI framework with full national coverage: the result may be a law, a law without the copyright chapter, or a postponement until 2027. The contrast is sharp: while these institutions move forward, the United States still has no oversight mechanism for the most advanced models, and the European Union still has the formal ratification of its regulatory package pending.
