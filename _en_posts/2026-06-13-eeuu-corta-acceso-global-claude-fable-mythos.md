On June 12, the US Department of Commerce did something unprecedented: it used an export control to order Anthropic to cut off access to two of its most powerful artificial intelligence models, Claude Fable 5 and Mythos 5. Since the company cannot distinguish in real time between US and foreign users, it ended up switching off both models for the entire planet. The [directive and the company's response](https://anthropic.com/news/fable-mythos-access) mark the first time in history that a government has pressed the off switch on a frontier AI model from a private lab.

The stated reason, according to the [order signed by the Commerce Secretary](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/12/anthropic-disables-access-to-fable-5-and-mythos-5-to-comply-with-government-directive.html), is that an unidentified company reported having achieved a "jailbreak" of Mythos —that is, having circumvented its safety restrictions to activate capabilities the model should not offer. Anthropic describes the situation as a "likely misunderstanding": it maintains that the jailbreak would be narrow (it would only be triggered by asking the model to read code and fix its vulnerabilities) and not a universal flaw. The rest of the Claude models are not affected.

For Latin America, the episode is a concrete lesson in dependence. The region did not take part in the agreements surrounding these models, has no seat at the G7 that begins on June 15 and was not consulted before the cutoff. Overnight, it lost access to Anthropic's most capable tools without prior notice. Europe completes the paradox: this same week it presented a 420 billion euro package to ensure that "no one has a kill switch" over its technology infrastructure. The United States has just shown that its own works.
