## In 60 seconds

- **What happened.** On September 23, the UN Security Council heard from Yoshua Bengio and the heads of OpenAI, Anthropic and Hugging Face.
- **Why it matters.** The common testing standards that Anthropic proposed could define how the models that governments later buy and regulate are evaluated.
- **What we don't know yet.** Whether the session produces any Council text; the record lists positions, and the United States rejected moving toward global governance.

The UN Security Council devoted a high-level session to artificial intelligence on September 23, convened and chaired by France, according to [the official UN record](https://press.un.org/en/sc/16462.doc.htm). The speakers were Yoshua Bengio, co-chair of the Independent International Scientific Panel on AI, and the CEOs of OpenAI, Anthropic and Hugging Face. Anthropic's Dario Amodei proposed narrow agreements, such as banning the use of AI to develop biological weapons, as well as verification systems and common testing standards. Clément Delangue said that Hugging Face was the first company to publicly disclose a cyberattack by an autonomous agent.

States brought different positions. The United States said that international dialogue cannot drift toward global governance. China offered 5,000 AI training opportunities for developing countries. Panama called for AI to remain under human direction, oversight and control, and Colombia warned that countries of the Global South that do not take part in producing AI will face a gap.

In our judgment, the practical point lies in the testing standards. As [The Information reported, cited by BankInfoSecurity](https://www.bankinfosecurity.com/google-openai-anthropic-plan-frontier-ai-standards-body-a-32926), Google, OpenAI and Anthropic are preparing a body that would set common evaluation standards for frontier models. If that body materializes, the companies being evaluated would design the evaluator, and a government that buys or regulates these systems would inherit tests it did not help define. On the other hand, a common standard among labs can be ready long before any agreement among states that do not even agree on the goal.
