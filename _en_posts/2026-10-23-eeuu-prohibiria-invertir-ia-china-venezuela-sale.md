## In 60 seconds

- **What happened.** On October 22, the US Treasury proposed barring US investors from financing entities that develop AI in China, Russia, Iran and other countries.
- **Why it matters.** Anyone with projects in Venezuela depends on an interpretation by the Treasury, and the open comment period is their way to ask for it to be written down.
- **What we don't know yet.** What final text the Treasury will adopt after the comment period, and whether Venezuela's exclusion will be written into the rule itself.

The [Treasury's fact sheet](https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/206/Fact-Sheet-FAQs-COINS-Act-NPRM.pdf) names the sectors covered: semiconductors, superintelligence systems, quantum, supercomputing, hypersonic systems and biotechnology. In them, US persons will have to notify certain investments in entities linked to countries of concern and will be barred from making others. For AI, the [proposed rule](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0667) bars investing in entities from those countries that develop any superintelligence system. That is what Executive Order 14434, signed on September 29, calls AI, as reported [on October 5](https://dobleclick.jaguridi.cl/en/2026/10/05/argentina-chile-firman-kioto-sin-montos-plazos.html).

The proposal implements the COINS Act, passed as part of the defense bill for fiscal year 2026, which mandates controls on US investment in countries of concern. The list includes China, Cuba, Iran, North Korea, Russia and Maduro's Venezuela. On this last case, the Treasury holds that Venezuela no longer meets the definition since the United States recognized Delcy Rodríguez on March 5. Transactions of up to $50,000 are exempt, and comments will be accepted until December 7.

For a fund or a company with projects in Venezuela, the practical difference lies in where that exit is written down. As long as it is a reading by the Treasury against a law that names the Maduro government, it could change with a new administrative criterion. The Venezuelan government and those investors could use the comment period to ask for the exclusion to appear in the final text. Because the term covers any AI system, the prohibition could reach any AI developer in the other countries on the list.

## In the region

In Brazil, the mayor of Porto Alegre, Sebastião Melo, signed [Law No. 14,644/2026](https://prefeitura.poa.br/gp/noticias/sancionada-lei-que-estabelece-regras-para-uso-de-inteligencia-artificial) on October 22. It requires the municipal administration to label content made with generative AI. It also prohibits solely automated decisions by high-risk systems with legal effects without human review, and requires a published algorithmic impact assessment for those systems. The executive branch has 180 days to issue implementing regulations.

Before the Superior Electoral Court, the Electoral Public Prosecutor's Office said Meta failed to comply with part of the October 15 injunction. Among other things, it did not report Meta AI statistics or filters on requests that mention each candidate, according to [JOTA](https://www.jota.info/eleicoes/eleicoes-2026/mpe-diz-que-meta-descumpriu-parte-das-decisoes-do-tse-e-ve-indicios-de-quebra-de-isonomia-nas-eleicoes).
