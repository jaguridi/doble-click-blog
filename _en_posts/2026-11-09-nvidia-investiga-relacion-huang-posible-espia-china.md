## In 60 seconds

- **What happened.** Nvidia's board announced on November 8 that it will investigate the relationship between Jensen Huang, its chief executive, and a former Megaspeed executive.
- **Why it matters.** The board defended Huang before having any results, and the report from its independent directors will show shareholders whether that defense holds up.
- **What we don't know yet.** Whether Huang will appear before three House committees at the closed-door session they requested for November 18.

A memo that circulated among U.S. officials this year says China's Ministry of State Security recruited Alice Huang to spy on Jensen Huang. She was an executive at Megaspeed, a chip company based in Singapore. That memo already appeared in the [November 8](https://dobleclick.jaguridi.cl/en/2026/11/08/peru-acordo-eeuu-centros-datos-sin-reglas.html) entry. Now Nvidia has announced an [investigation](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/08/nvidia-says-its-investigating-ceo-jensen-huangs-relationship-with-possible-chinese-spy/) to be led by independent directors with outside legal counsel. The same day, Huang admitted in a message to staff that he had been unfaithful to his wife.

According to TechCrunch, the board said it has no reason to believe that national security or the company's secrets were compromised. It added that it has confidence in Huang. That conclusion came before any findings from the investigators. If the report contradicts it, the board would be exposed to its shareholders for having gotten ahead of it. We did not have the board's statement in hand: what is quoted comes from TechCrunch.

The U.S. Congress had already moved. On November 5, the chairs of the House Foreign Affairs, China, and Intelligence committees [asked Huang](https://foreignaffairs.house.gov/news/press-releases/chairmen-mast-moolenaar-and-crawford-request-jensen-huang-brief-congress-on-link-to-chinese-intelligence-asset) to brief them behind closed doors. Because the session is closed, what he says there may not be made public.

## In the region

In Brazil, ByteDance is looking for a developer for the second phase of its data center complex in Pecém, in Ceará. Bloomberg reported it on November 6, in a story republished by [InvestNews](https://investnews.com.br/negocios/dona-do-tiktok-negocia-nova-fase-de-complexo-de-data-centers-no-ceara/) (via InvestNews). The expansion could exceed the project under construction, worth $10 billion and 300 megawatts. The company is also in preliminary talks for another center, outside Pecém, that would serve the Brazilian market. According to the story, the Pecém centers must export data to receive tax exemptions. ByteDance did not respond to Bloomberg.

The same [Octoverse 2026](https://github.blog/news-insights/octoverse/octoverse-commits-pushes-reach-new-highs-in-2026-as-developers-build-with-agents/) report counts 10.2 million developers in Brazil.
