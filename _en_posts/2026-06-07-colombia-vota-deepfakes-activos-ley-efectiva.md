Colombia is 15 days away from its presidential runoff (Espriella against Cepeda, on June 21) with a formal statement from Inspector General Gregorio Eljach: ["disinformation represents the main risk factor"](https://www.eltiempo.com/politica/elecciones-colombia-2026/las-fake-news-crecen-como-la-principal-amenaza-en-la-antesala-de-la-segunda-vuelta-3562646) for the electoral process. The alert is not abstract: deepfakes with the Gemini logo visible are circulating on social media, cloned audio is being distributed via WhatsApp for as little as $10, and fake emails imitate the National Civil Registry. The problem is one of timing: anti-deepfake Law 2502 has no effective enforcement until July, more than ten days after the vote.

The scenario places Colombia as the most urgent test case of democracy and artificial intelligence in Latin America in 2026: 41.4 million voters face a presidential election without any institutional mechanism equivalent to Brazil's Superior Electoral Court to respond in real time to generative disinformation. Brazil took three years to build that framework, and its AI law (PL 2338) has just been approved. Colombia, without that scaffolding, is going to vote first.
