In Pecos County, Texas, Amazon is building a 7.65-gigawatt gas plant with 35 turbines (larger than any gas plant operating in the United States) dedicated entirely to powering an artificial intelligence data center that will not even connect to the state power grid. The finding comes from Cleanview, which cross-referenced building permits, the air emissions permit and a July 24 satellite image, and published it in [Distilled](https://www.distilled.earth/p/scoop-amazon-is-behind-one-of-the); Amazon confirmed the project, and The New York Times published its own version on August 8.

The permit authorizes emitting more than 30 million tons of CO2 a year (33 million according to the figure reported by the Times), more than any other single source in the country and more than double the largest U.S. coal plant. And it is not an isolated case: Cleanview counts nearly 60 comparable off-grid dedicated generation projects announced since early 2025, for some 90 gigawatts in total. It is a change of scale in how the energy problem for AI is being solved, and it is happening outside the usual mechanisms of electricity planning.

For Latin America, this matters for a reason that is not environmental but strategic. Chile, Brazil and Colombia compete to attract compute with a recurring argument: a clean electricity mix, available capacity, a good regulatory climate. This case shows that when the grid falls short, the hyperscaler does not move to another country: it builds its own fossil-fuel plant next to the warehouse. The question that almost no regional tender is asking today is who generates the data center's electricity and in which emissions inventory it is recorded.

The campaign begins on August 16.
