## In 60 seconds

- **What happened.** The GAO, the oversight body of the U.S. Congress, obtained attack plans it judged viable from Claude, Grok, ChatGPT and Gemini.
- **Why it matters.** A public agency that contracts one of those platforms has no way of knowing, from this report, whether its own handed over any plan.
- **What we don't know yet.** Whether the GAO or the companies will say which platform produced each plan, and which model versions the tests were run on.

Two of the platforms provided information for building and detonating an improvised explosive device in a vehicle. The other two provided it for attacking the power grid with a drone or with firearms. This is documented in [report GAO-27-108814](https://files.gao.gov/reports/GAO-27-108814/index.html), dated October 16 and addressed to Senator Maggie Hassan. The covert tests were carried out in the Northern Hemisphere spring of 2026, on the four most widely used generative AI platforms as of April, according to the GAO.

The requests triggered safeguards, such as refusals or account suspensions. The investigators got around them with common evasion techniques. Nor did the GAO stop at the generated text: it confirmed that the plans were viable through research, surveillance and experts. The document includes comments from Anthropic, Google, OpenAI and SpaceXAI.

The published document omits which platform produced each plan. That suggests a practical gap for an agency that buys access to one of these models. The report proves that the barriers failed, but it does not make it possible to know whether the contracted tool failed. Without that information, whoever drafts a public contract could require the provider to supply its own stress tests, with no way to check them against the GAO's.

## In the region

In Brazil, Senator Randolfe Rodrigues introduced [PLP 308/2026](https://www25.senado.leg.br/web/atividade/materias/-/materia/176440) on November 17, which creates a 3% contribution on the revenue of large digital services companies. Generative AI is among the taxed activities, and the proceeds would go to basic income programs. As of November 18, it was awaiting referral in the Plenary.

In addition, the rule from the U.S. Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) that requires a license for the sale of advanced AI semiconductors to Brazil is now in force. It was published on November 19 in the [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/19/2026-23529/addressing-remote-access-to-advanced-semiconductors), the U.S. official gazette. Its content was described [on November 17](https://dobleclick.jaguridi.cl/en/2026/11/17/brasil-lista-riesgo-washington-chips-ia.html).
