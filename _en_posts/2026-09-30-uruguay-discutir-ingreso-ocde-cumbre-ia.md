## In 60 seconds

- **What happened.** On September 29, at an OECD summit on AI in Montevideo, Minister Gabriel Oddone said that Uruguay is beginning to discuss membership.
- **Why it matters.** The OECD's projection on AI and productivity covers only its Latin American members, and Uruguay, which co-chairs the regional program, is not yet one.
- **What we don't know yet.** What the ministers agree on during the second day, September 30, and whether Uruguay takes any formal step toward accession.

Artificial intelligence could raise labor productivity by between 13% and 53% over the next decade in the Latin American member countries of the OECD. That was the projection from Mathias Cormann, secretary-general of the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development, according to [Ámbito](https://www.ambito.com/uruguay/comienza-discutir-su-ingreso-la-ocde-y-apuesta-la-inteligencia-artificial-disparar-la-productividad-n6328207). He made the remarks at the opening of the eleventh Ministerial Summit of the OECD Latin America and the Caribbean Regional Programme, devoted to inclusive innovation and AI. The meeting began in Montevideo and ends on September 30, according to [the official page](https://www.oecd-events.org/e/lacrp10/en/content/ministerial-summit). The figure is an OECD estimate, known for now through the press.

Cormann also said, according to the same outlet, that the region's productivity per hour is equivalent to 38% of the organization's average. Set against that gap, a range running from 13% to 53% suggests that the effect of AI is far from automatic.

Uruguay and Costa Rica co-chair the program, according to [Uruguay's Ministry of Economy and Finance](https://www.gub.uy/ministerio-economia-finanzas/comunicacion/noticias/uruguay-condiciones-compartir-experiencias-exitosas-senalo-ministro-oddone). At the summit, its minister, Gabriel Oddone, told [Ámbito](https://www.ambito.com/uruguay/comienza-discutir-su-ingreso-la-ocde-y-apuesta-la-inteligencia-artificial-disparar-la-productividad-n6328207) that OECD membership "is a path that Uruguay is beginning to discuss." If that discussion moves forward, the host country could go from leading the regional program to joining the group of members that projections like Cormann's are aimed at.
