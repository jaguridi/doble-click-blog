French startup Mistral AI is in talks to raise about 3 billion euros at a valuation close to 20 billion, as [revealed by Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-12/france-s-mistral-in-funding-talks-at-about-20-billion-valuation). The figure nearly doubles what it was worth just nine months ago, and it comes at a very particular moment: the round is being negotiated just as Anthropic's two most advanced models remain switched off across the planet by order of the US government. Mistral, which presents itself as the "sovereign" European option against the US duopoly, thus finds a sales argument handed to it on a silver platter.

The idea of technological "sovereignty" stopped being abstract this week. Sovereignty, here, means something very concrete: that no outsider can switch off the infrastructure you depend on. Europe has spent days insisting that it wants to make sure "no one has a kill switch" over its digital tools, and Mistral is the corporate face of that ambition. The detail that makes all this relevant for Latin America is that Mistral already operates commercially in Colombia, Chile, Mexico, Brazil and Argentina. In other words: the region has at hand, today, an alternative that does not depend on Washington, at the very moment it found out what it costs to depend on it.

It is worth not idealizing it: a higher valuation does not by itself mean better models or real independence, and raising money at that scale also ties Mistral to its own investors and geographies. But the move sketches a more plural map, where frontier AI stops being an exclusively US affair and begins to have European accents —and, by way of commercial availability, Latin American ones too.

## Threads we're following

The backdrop to all this is the story we have been following since June 12: that day, an order from the US Department of Commerce forced Anthropic to [switch off its Claude Fable 5 and Mythos 5 models](https://www.anthropic.com/news/fable-mythos-access) for the whole world, in the first case of a government using an export control as an off switch on a frontier AI model. Three days later they still have no return date; presidential AI adviser David Sacks backed the directive, while Anthropic insists that the "jailbreak" that prompted the measure is narrow and that the standard, if generalized, would halt all of the industry's deployments. That void —that of a region that overnight lost access to tools it used daily, without having been consulted— is exactly the gap that an alternative like Mistral aims to fill.
