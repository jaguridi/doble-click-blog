## In 60 seconds

- **What happened.** A São Paulo court barred Meta from crawling the websites of media outlets that belong to three Brazilian industry associations, or from training Meta AI on their content.
- **Why it matters.** The protection covers the members of those associations: an unaffiliated outlet remains exposed to the terms that take effect on November 20.
- **What we don't know yet.** How Meta will respond within its five-day deadline, and whether it will appeal the measure before its new terms take effect.

The fine is one million reais per day, capped at 100 million. It is set by [the order of the 44th Civil Court (44ª Vara Cível) of the Central Forum of São Paulo](https://static.poder360.com.br/uploads/2026/11/despacho-justica-sp-3nov2026-1.pdf), signed on November 3 against Facebook Serviços Online do Brasil. It was requested by ABERT, ANJ and ANER, the industry associations for that sector. The measure suspends, for their members, the final part of Clause 1 of Meta's new Commercial Terms. According to the associations, that clause makes their accounts conditional on allowing automated collection from their websites.

The court orders that their accounts on Instagram, Facebook, WhatsApp and Threads be kept active, without retaliation. A member could thus refuse crawling without losing its accounts while the measure is in force. Meta has five days to respond. It had not responded to [Poder360](https://www.poder360.com.br/poder-justica/justica-de-sp-proibe-meta-de-usar-conteudo-jornalistico-para-treinar-ia/) by the time that outlet published its story.

The order protects only the members of those three associations. A digital outlet that does not belong to them would fall under the general terms when they take effect, unless it obtains its own measure. Litigation over training models on protected works had, in Europe, [the Munich ruling in favor of GEMA against Suno](https://dobleclick.jaguridi.cl/en/2026/08/01/modelos-salieron-del-laboratorio-atacaron-sistemas-reales.html), reported on August 1. In São Paulo it arrives by another route: a platform contract, not a copyright infringement lawsuit.

## In the region

In the Dominican Republic, Decree 787-26 approved on November 3 an AI Code of Ethics that is mandatory for the public administration, according to [the Presidency](https://presidencia.gob.do/noticias/el-gobierno-aprueba-el-codigo-de-etica-de-la-inteligencia-artificial-para-garantizar-un). It prohibits social scoring and mass biometric identification in public spaces without a court order. Systems that affect rights or social benefits will require a prior impact assessment, and the State has 180 days to comply. As of November 4, the text of the decree could not be located, only the press release.

In Chile, the Ministry General Secretariat of the Presidency (Segpres) coordinates the government's legislative agenda. Together with the Finance Ministry, it objects to Minister Ximena Lincolao's amendment to the AI bill, which eliminates the risk categories, according to [La Tercera](https://www.latercera.com/nacional/noticia/la-ley-de-ia-que-ximena-lincolao-reescribio-sola-y-que-ahora-segpres-intenta-corregir-antes-de-enviarla-al-congreso/). The Finance Ministry also rejects a 100% accelerated depreciation for technology companies. The Segpres is reviewing the text before sending it to the Senate, where the proposed deadline for amendments is December 4.
