## In 60 seconds

- **What happened.** Mia Mottley, prime minister of Barbados, said her country will regulate AI on its own if it does not receive CARICOM's regional report in February.
- **Why it matters.** The commission that Caribbean heads of government agreed on in July now has a public deadline, set by one of its own members.
- **What we don't know yet.** Whether that commission will deliver its report on time, and what clauses a possible national framework in Barbados would include.

The warning came at the closing press conference of the Special Summit on AI Safety. Barbados and the United Nations held it on November 23 in Bridgetown. The deadline is aimed at the commission that the heads of government of the Caribbean Community (CARICOM), the bloc that coordinates policy among Caribbean governments, agreed on in July. The date is known through the local press, which published it in [Barbados Today](https://barbadostoday.bb/2026/11/23/barbados-sets-february-deadline-for-caricom-ai-framework/). During the summit, Mottley also put forward two ideas, according to [UN News](https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/11/1168861). One makes the lab responsible when its agent, an AI program that carries out actions on its own, attacks a Barbadian bank. The other would allow the government to suspend the use of those models during an emergency.

At the same summit, António Guterres reiterated in his [speech](https://press.un.org/en/2026/sgsm23382.doc.htm) the proposal for a multilateral AI risk management framework, with credible and independent oversight. He also returned to a Global Fund for AI Capacity Development. According to [AFP, cited by France 24](https://www.france24.com/en/americas/20261123-un-chief-antonio-guterres-calls-for-global-ai-safety-framework-within-a-year), he called for developing that framework within the next year. The secretary-general said the AI capacity network the UN supports has 27 centers of excellence in five regions.

A deadline with a name and a date could shift the position of the bloc's other governments. If Barbados legislates first, the rest could debate the regional approach against a national rule already underway, rather than from scratch. Mottley's ideas suggest what that rule could bring: provider liability for the harms caused by its agents and a power to suspend the service. Both could be set in a national law without waiting for the multilateral agreement Guterres proposes.

## In the region

In Colombia, AWS, Amazon's cloud, opened a Local Zone in Bogotá on November 20, according to its [announcement](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/11/aws-local-zone-bogota-colombia/). It allows data to be processed within the country with compute, storage and container services. The company promises single-digit millisecond latencies for workloads such as medical imaging and financial transactions.

In Brazil, Anthropic and the Aliança de IA para a Educação announced up to $10 million to test AI in public schools. The alliance is made up of Fundação Lemann, Telles Foundation and VélezReyes+, and the first pilots will take place in 2027, [Teletime](https://teletime.com.br/23/11/2026/anthropic-alianca-ia-para-educacao/) reported (via Teletime).
