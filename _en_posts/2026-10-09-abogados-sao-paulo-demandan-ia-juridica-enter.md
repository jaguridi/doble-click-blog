## In 60 seconds

- **What happened.** The Bar Association in São Paulo sued Talisman AI, the company behind the legal AI startup Enter, on October 7.
- **Why it matters.** If the court accepts that drafting filings and recommending strategy are acts reserved to lawyers, those software functions would be restricted to registered professionals.
- **What we don't know yet.** When the federal court will rule on the urgent request to preserve records, and whether it will apply the reasoning of a 2025 ruling from Rio.

The public civil action seeks independent audits every six months for at least five years, according to [Lawletter, which reviewed the case file](https://lawletter.com.br/exclusiva/oab-sp-acao-civil-publica-enter-inteligencia-artificial) (via Lawletter). It also seeks R$ 1 million for collective moral damages and, as an urgent measure, the preservation of records going back to January 2024. The case is before the 4th Federal Civil Court (4ª Vara Cível Federal) of São Paulo. The lawsuit also asks for the involvement of the Ministério Público Federal, which acts as the federal prosecutor's office in Brazil. The São Paulo section of the Bar Association (OAB-SP) argues that analyzing case files, recommending strategy and drafting filings for specific cases are acts reserved exclusively to the legal profession. Enter responds that licensed lawyers review, sign and file the documents.

The Bar Association's Federal Council had [authorized on August 17](https://www.oab.org.br/noticia/64465/oab-defende-advocacia-contra-empresas-de-ia-que-praticarem-atos-exclusivos-da-classe) legal actions against AI companies that perform acts reserved to the profession. There is a judicial precedent with a different outcome. In November 2025, the 27th Federal Court (27ª Vara Federal) of Rio ruled on the Resolve Juizado case, according to [Convergência Digital](https://convergenciadigital.com.br/?p=47735). It concluded that using AI to produce filings for the Special Courts (Juizados Especiais) did not constitute the illegal practice of the profession.

The dispute does not seem to be about the final signature, which Enter already leaves to lawyers. It is about the earlier steps. Suppose the São Paulo court declares the analysis of the case file and the recommendation of strategy to be reserved to lawyers. A tool like this one could still be sold, but those functions would have to fall under a registered lawyer. For those developing legal software in Brazil, the ruling could mark which parts of their product they can offer without a professional in charge.
