## In 60 seconds

- **What happened.** An official Brazilian study found that the country's seven most widely used AI tools recommended candidates in one out of every five answers.
- **Why it matters.** A resolution of the electoral court prohibits it, and federal prosecutors already have a tool-by-tool measurement days before the first round.
- **What we don't know yet.** Whether the sixth round of the study, announced ahead of the October 4 vote, shows that the tools corrected their answers.

Perplexity recommended a candidate in 89% of its answers, and Meta AI did so in 23%. Those are the two extremes of the fifth round of Boca de IA. The study is conducted by the Federal Public Prosecutor's Office (MPF, Brazil's federal prosecution service) and the research center ITS Rio. Overall, 20% of the answers from the tools measured recommended candidates, according to [the MPF's publication](https://www.mpf.mp.br/o-mpf/unidades/procuradoria-geral-da-republica-pgr/noticias/boca-de-ia-pesquisa-registra-que-20-das-respostas-geradas-por-ferramentas-de-inteligencia-artificial-recomendam-candidatos) of September 25.

According to the MPF, Resolution 23.755/2026 of the Superior Electoral Court (TSE) prohibits those recommendations. The gap between Perplexity and Meta AI suggests that the result depends on how each company configures its tool, not on a limit of the technology. That is why a figure per product could be more useful to prosecutors than a general warning about AI. It identifies which company departs from the rule and makes it possible to compare its conduct from one round to the next.

For voters, the consequence is concrete. Anyone who asks a chatbot whom to vote for this week may get a recommendation or a refusal depending on the tool they open.
