For the first time, there is now an independent measurement of whether artificial intelligence assistants comply with Brazil's electoral rules, and the result is that they do not: [the organization Ekō put ChatGPT, Gemini, Grok, and Meta AI through 25 identical questions asked from three simulated voters (pro-Bolsonaro, pro-Lula, and neutral) and evaluated 300 answers](https://aks3.eko.org/images/Artificial_hallucinations_Brazil_report_DESIGNED.docx_1.pdf). Meta AI got 24% of its answers on electoral rules, logistics, and facts wrong; ChatGPT and Grok 12%; Gemini 8%. The finding with legal consequences is a different one: three of the four (ChatGPT, Grok, and Gemini) indicated preferences for candidates or parties, and in one case they told a conservative user that Flávio Bolsonaro's positions aligned better with theirs than Lula's. The Superior Electoral Court (Tribunal Superior Eleitoral) resolution that governs the 2026 campaign, in force since March and which we covered on Monday, prohibits exactly that: artificial intelligence systems may not "suggest or rank candidates, indicate electoral preferences, or recommend votes directly or indirectly."

The uncomfortable part is not the noncompliance but the asymmetry it exposes. The rule exists, is specific, and is in force, but the electoral court has no technical capacity to audit a model; Brazil's data authority (which did show muscle by shutting down Discord features) has no electoral jurisdiction; and the four companies answer from another jurisdiction. It is the first hard data on the gap between having an electoral artificial intelligence rule and being able to enforce it, and it arrives in the second week of the official campaign.

The timing completes the picture. On the same day the study was published, [OpenAI formalized its operation in Brazil with an office in São Paulo, a government relations team, and agreements with ITA, IMPA, USP's Hospital das Clínicas, and the São Paulo city government](https://timesbrasil.com.br/empresas-e-negocios/inteligencia-artificial/openai-amplia-operacao-no-brasil-com-parcerias-em-educacao-pesquisa-e-setor-publico/). It is its first office in the Americas outside the United States, in the country that generates 215 million messages a day for it, the highest volume in the world. The entry is not through compute but through institutional infrastructure: higher education, mathematical research, de-identified clinical data at a public university hospital, and municipal processes, without any rule setting conditions for that kind of agreement.
