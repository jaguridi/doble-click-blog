Brazil has just crossed a line no Latin American country had crossed: on May 27, the Chamber of Deputies approved bill 2338/2023, the region's first artificial intelligence legal framework with full national scope ([IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/b/brazils-chamber-of-deputies-approves-rules-on-ai-use)). The text is modeled on the European AI regulation: it classifies systems according to their risk level, creates a National AI Regulation and Governance System and sets fines that can reach 50 million reais.

Two unknowns remain that define its real scope. The first is whether the text kept the copyright chapter that came from the Senate (more than fifty organizations from the creative world warned about its possible removal in the rapporteur's version). The second is procedural: whether the law goes straight to presidential enactment or returns to the Senate with amendments, which could stretch the process out by several months. The detail matters because it sets the pace: with direct enactment, Brazil would have a law before the October elections and the rest of the region would have a concrete model to learn from. The timing stands out because of the contrast: the milestone comes just as the United States has been left without any public oversight mechanism for frontier models (its executive order was permanently scrapped on May 21) and as the European Union postponed its own deadlines for high-risk systems. At least in Brazil, the region is filling that vacuum with a standard of its own.

## In the region

The approval of PL 2338 is the most significant regulatory event Latin America has experienced so far in 2026, and it suddenly opens two debates that were already on the table. The first is the tension between AI and creators over copyright, a discussion Chile also faced in May. The second is how to govern high-risk systems in a scenario where the sector's leading power has just dismantled its own public oversight. For those who want to have a say, there is an open window: the European Commission is holding a consultation on the classification of high-risk systems until June 23, and organizations in the region can submit comments.
