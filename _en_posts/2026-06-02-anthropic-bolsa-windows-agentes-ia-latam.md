Anthropic [filed its confidential S-1 with the SEC](https://www.anthropic.com/news/confidential-draft-s1-sec) on June 1, the first formal step toward an IPO planned for October 2026, at a valuation close to $965 billion. The same day, Microsoft held its Build 2026 conference in San Francisco and announced that Windows will become the first massive-scale platform to natively run artificial intelligence agents (programs that act autonomously to complete tasks).

The most striking announcement at Build was Project Polaris, Microsoft's own model, which will replace GPT-4 in GitHub Copilot starting in August. For Latin America, where Microsoft dominates more than 80% of government and business computers, this means that the most widespread AI-assisted programming tool in the corporate sector will stop depending on OpenAI. In parallel, the Windows Agent Framework (released as open source) turns the region's more than 200 million Windows PCs into potential execution nodes for AI agents with no need for additional cloud infrastructure. Satya Nadella summed it up on stage: "agents are not a feature."

Anthropic's S-1 also has a regional dimension: when the document is made public (estimated for between July and August) it will be the first time investors can see the full numbers of a frontier AI company. Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley are listed as underwriters of the process; the price and number of shares have not yet been determined.

## In the region

Colombia heads to the June 21 presidential runoff with anti-deepfake Law 2502 in force, but without the penalties for the use of AI being active: they take effect in July, when the most sensitive electoral period will already be over. It is the first real test in Latin America of an electoral AI rule with an approved law but no effective enforcement at the moment it matters most. What happens there will be a precedent for the whole region. In parallel, the dismantling of Colorado's law under pressure from tech lobbying confirms a pattern Latin American regulators already know: the benchmark regulatory model will remain the European EU AI Act, not the US one. Also still pending is President Lula's signature on Brazil's PL 2338 (approved by the Chamber on May 27) and whether the copyright chapter, which requires compensation for the creators of content used to train models, survived into the final text.
