## In 60 seconds

- **What happened.** Pope Leo XIV called on the states that will meet in Geneva in November to move toward banning lethal autonomous weapons.
- **Why it matters.** It adds support to the call for a treaty that Brazil led with 39 countries, in a forum where consensus allowed the United States and Russia to block it.
- **What we don't know yet.** Whether the Review Conference in November will open a mandate to negotiate a binding instrument or accept another extension of the talks.

Decisions about human life, freedom and the use of force must remain under effective, informed and accountable human control. Leo XIV proposed this on October 30 at the Apostolic Palace, among five considerations, according to [the speech published by the Holy See Press Office](https://press.vatican.va/content/salastampa/es/bollettino/pubblico/2026/10/30/301026e.html). He spoke before a meeting on the future of AI organized by the Pontifical Academy for Life and the Future of Life Institute. There he encouraged states to "take concrete steps toward the prohibition of lethal autonomous weapons." He also said that AI must be disarmed, quoting his encyclical Magnifica humanitas.

The speech rejects granting rights or legal personhood to AI systems. That refusal suggests that, when a system causes harm, the claim would still be directed at a person or at a named institution. Along the same lines, the pope called for international institutions with shared rules, independent oversight, accountability and redress for harm.

The call comes on an open file. On September 7, 128 states concluded a non-binding framework on these weapons in Geneva, as we reported [that day](https://dobleclick.jaguridi.cl/en/2026/09/07/primer-acuerdo-global-armas-autonomas.html). In that round, the United States and Russia blocked a treaty, and a declaration by 39 countries, led by Brazil, called for negotiating a binding instrument. Because decisions in that forum are made by consensus, papal backing is not enough to lift a veto. It could, however, give the Brazilian delegation an argument to demand that negotiations be opened.

## In the region

In Chile, the National Center for Artificial Intelligence (CENIA) signed a two-year memorandum with Microsoft for Latam GPT. The center presents it as an open Latin American language model. According to [CENIA](https://cenia.cl/2026/10/29/microsoft-y-cenia-le-dan-motor-a-latam-gpt-el-primer-gran-modelo-de-lenguaje-abierto-de-america-latina/), the agreement announced on October 28 includes $250,000 in credits for Azure, Microsoft's cloud, through February 2027. They are in-kind credits, not cash.

In Peru, President Keiko Fujimori announced that the government will gradually promote telemedicine, with no timeline or budget, according to [the Presidency](https://www.gob.pe/institucion/presidencia/noticias/1466258-gobierno-impulsara-la-telemedicina-para-reducir-tiempos-de-espera-en-el-sistema-de-salud-anuncia-presidenta-fujimori). She said so after seeing the Doctor SV platform in El Salvador, where she also announced [500 schools connected by satellite internet](https://www.gob.pe/institucion/presidencia/noticias/1466203-presidenta-fujimori-conocio-experiencia-de-el-salvador-en-modernizacion-de-infraestructura-escolar-e-innovacion-educativa) next year.
