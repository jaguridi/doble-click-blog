## In 60 seconds

- **What happened.** On November 16, the United States announced a rule that requires a license to export advanced AI chips to Brazil and four other countries.
- **Why it matters.** Brazilian-owned cloud operators will have to wait for an individual authorization, which subsidiaries with a U.S.-headquartered parent that report are spared.
- **What we don't know yet.** When the rule will appear in the Federal Register, the official journal whose publication starts its validity, and whether the Brazilian government will submit comments.

Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, and Thailand complete the list, and Brazil is the only Latin American country on it. So says the [interim final rule](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-23529.pdf) from the Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS), the Commerce Department office that controls sensitive exports. The rule considers that in those destinations the risk of chip diversion is higher than normal. No license is needed if the recipient or its parent company is headquartered in the United States and the exporter meets reporting obligations.

The rule also reaches data centers in any country. A license is required for those that supply significant volumes of compute to companies headquartered in China, Macau, or other destinations in group D:5, a category in U.S. regulations. Those that were already doing so have 90 days from the entry into force, according to the [BIS statement](https://www.bis.gov/press-release/bis-issues-rule-addressing-remote-access-american-super-intelligence-technology).

That exemption separates two classes of operators within Brazil, and the sector has already noticed. For Abes, a Brazilian trade association of software companies, the rule creates an "assimetria competitiva" (competitive asymmetry), according to [Teletime](https://teletime.com.br/16/11/2026/eua-chips-ia-data-centers-brasil/). The text adds that, when deciding each application, BIS will look at how effective the destination country's control system is and how much it cooperates with the agency. Docket BIS-2026-0628 accepts comments for 60 days. That is where the Ministry of Development, Industry, Trade and Services (MDIC) and Itamaraty, Brazil's foreign ministry, could submit observations.

## In the region

The calculation comes from its Secretariat for Economic Reforms, according to [Agência Brasil](https://agenciabrasil.ebc.com.br/economia/noticia/2026-11/regulacao-de-big-techs-pode-reduzir-custos-em-ate-r-50-bilhoes). As of 06:00 that day it had not been voted on, and [Teletime](https://teletime.com.br/16/11/2026/cgi-br-regulacao-de-plataformas/) speaks of a possible vote this week.
