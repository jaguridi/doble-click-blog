## In 60 seconds

- **What happened.** An AI memorandum that Peru and the United States signed in September contemplates building digital infrastructure in Peru, including data centers.
- **Why it matters.** Peru lacks a specific rule on those facilities and their environmental impact, so projects could be processed with general permits alone.
- **What we don't know yet.** Which specific projects will be promoted under the agreement, and whether the Peruvian government will publish the official text with its work plans.

The agreement seeks to "facilitate commercial and business activities" between US AI providers and the Peruvian public and private sectors. That is how it is quoted by [OjoPúblico](https://ojo-publico.com/6578/memorandum-ia-eeuu-busca-construir-mas-centros-datos-peru), which reported on November 8 that it had obtained exclusive access to the document. The text had not been made public since it was signed. According to the outlet, Peru has no specific regulation on data centers or on their possible environmental impacts.

The signing was reported on September 8 in [El Peruano](https://elperuano.pe/noticia/304306-pcm-y-estados-unidos-acuerdan-impulsar-el-desarrollo-de-la-ia-en-el-peru). According to that newspaper, it was signed by the President of the Council of Ministers, Luis Galarreta, and Ambassador Bernie Navarro. The memorandum covers regulation, cloud, data centers, 5G (the fifth generation of mobile networks), cybersecurity and training, and is in force for three years. Its Peruvian point of contact is the Secretariat of Government and Digital Transformation, part of the Presidency of the Council of Ministers (PCM). As of November 8, no PCM statement on the text could be found on gob.pe.

That three-year term sets the relevant timeframe. If initiatives of that kind arrive during that period, they could be evaluated under the general permits in force, unless the Congress of the Republic first approves a rule of its own. The Secretariat, as coordinator, is the body that could publish the text and show Congress and civil society which projects are being promoted. What is described of the content comes from the press, without the official document in view.
