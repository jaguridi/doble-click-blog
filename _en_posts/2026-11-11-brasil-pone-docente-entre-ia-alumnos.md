## In 60 seconds

- **What happened.** Brazil's Ministry of Education approved on November 10 the National Education Council's guidelines on the use of AI in teaching.
- **Why it matters.** School systems that already use platforms with features that are now banned will have to review those contracts, not just their classroom practices.
- **What we don't know yet.** Which body will enforce the platforms' compliance with the bans, and on what exact date the resolution that puts them into effect will be published.

From 6th to 9th grade, AI will only be allowed with a teacher's mediation. From 1st to 5th grade, generative AI (the kind that produces text or images on request) is not recommended for students. In early childhood education, students will not use it, not even with a teacher beside them. That is what [Agência Brasil](https://agenciabrasil.ebc.com.br/educacao/noticia/2026-11/mec-aprova-diretrizes-para-uso-de-ia-em-cada-etapa-da-educacao) reports about the Council's opinion that the Ministry of Education (MEC) approved.

The rule also sets limits on assessment. AI may play a complementary role in supporting the grading of essays, but grades remain the teacher's sole responsibility. Social scoring, emotion inference, continuous biometric surveillance and the use of educational data for advertising are banned. According to the agency, the bans apply from the publication of the resolution, and institutions have 12 months to bring their policies and contracts into line.

For school systems, the most concrete change could lie in what they have bought. A platform that today infers a student's mood or monitors them with biometric data would have to stop doing so. That obligation does not wait for the adjustment period. The education departments would then be the ones that must review which features their current contracts include. What is described here comes from the press: the opinion and the MEC's statement were not consulted.
