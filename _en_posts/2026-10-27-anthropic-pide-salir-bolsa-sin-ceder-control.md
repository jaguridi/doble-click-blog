## In 60 seconds

- **What happened.** On October 26, Anthropic filed its prospectus to list on the Nasdaq as ANT, still without a price or number of shares.
- **Why it matters.** Those who buy into the offering will not be able to change the company's leadership, because the cofounders retain the majority of the vote.
- **What we don't know yet.** On what date Anthropic will set the price range for the offering and how much money it will seek to raise with it on the Nasdaq.

The half-year revenue of $16.286 billion compares with $1.163 billion a year earlier, according to the [Form S-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/anthropicpbc-sx1.htm). That is the prospectus the US Securities and Exchange Commission requires before shares are sold to the public. The net loss for the period reached $80.7 billion. Of that total, $72.388 billion corresponds to the change in the fair value of convertible notes, derivatives and SAFEs, agreements in which an investor advances money in exchange for future shares. Most of the red ink is therefore an accounting adjustment on those instruments, and the operating loss came to $693 million. All of these figures are reported by the company itself.

The document adds that since January Anthropic has signed or expanded compute and infrastructure contracts with future minimum payments of about $417 billion. That sum is more than 25 times what it billed in the half year. This suggests that much of the cost is already locked in, while revenue will depend on how much its customers keep consuming.

The control structure is what matters most for a buyer. The Class F shares of the seven cofounders will hold 50.1% of the vote, and the Long-Term Benefit Trust will elect the majority of the board. A buyer of Class A shares will receive a stake in the business, but their votes would not be enough to replace those who run it. According to the prospectus, the offering is not being registered with the securities regulators of Brazil and Mexico. Those are the Securities and Exchange Commission of Brazil (CVM) and the National Banking and Securities Commission (CNBV).

## In the region

In Chile, the Senate's Future Challenges committee unanimously approved on October 26 the idea of legislating the AI bill, according to [La Tercera](https://www.latercera.com/nacional/noticia/incertidumbre-por-ley-ia-indicacion-del-gobierno-lleva-meses-lista-pero-aun-no-llega-al-congreso-y-tensiona-a-lincolao/). It set December 4 as the deadline for amendments, an agreement the full Senate must ratify. The Science Ministry's substitute amendment is ready, but its submission depends on the Segpres (the Ministry General Secretariat of the Presidency) and has no date.

Also in Chile, the Supreme Court approved an AI policy for the Judiciary. According to [La Tercera](https://www.latercera.com/nacional/noticia/corte-suprema-aprueba-politica-de-ia-no-se-podra-usar-para-decidir-casos-ni-valorar-la-prueba-y-menos-fundamentar-fallos/), which cites sources from the full court, AI will not be allowed to decide cases, weigh evidence or provide the reasoning for rulings. The text had not been published as of October 27.
