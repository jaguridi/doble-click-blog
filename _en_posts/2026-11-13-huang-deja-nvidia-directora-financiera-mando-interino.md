## In 60 seconds

- **What happened.** Jensen Huang left all his positions at Nvidia on November 12, and Colette Kress took over as interim chief executive officer.
- **Why it matters.** Two Democratic lawmakers asked the Commerce Department to preserve its records on chip sales to China, which could steer the case toward how those sales were authorized.
- **What we don't know yet.** Who will succeed Huang, what the company will say when it presents its results on November 17, and what date his testimony before the House will have.

Kress has been chief financial officer since September 2013 and will keep that position until a successor is named, according to [Nvidia's announcement](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-announces-ceo-transition). The board will look at internal and external candidates. It also appointed Harvey C. Jones, former chief executive officer of Synopsys, as its first chair. With both positions held by one person, that results release could give analysts an early opportunity to question Kress about Megaspeed and about committed orders.

On the same day, Ron Wyden and Richard Neal wrote to the Commerce Department. They are the ranking Democrats on the Senate Finance Committee and the House Ways and Means Committee. They asked it to preserve all its records on Nvidia's chip sales to China, according to their [statement](https://www.finance.senate.gov/ranking-members-news/wyden-and-neal-begin-to-investigate-administration-scheme-to-take-cut-of-nvidia-ai-chip-sales-to-chinese-companies-trump-and-commerce-department-reportedly-ignored-warnings-about-former-ceos/). The request covers the records of the export licenses and of the Section 232 tariff tied to those sales. Those documents would make it possible to reconstruct how the US government authorized those sales. If Commerce preserves them and later hands them over, the focus could shift from Nvidia's internal leadership to whether that permit system complied with the law.

The departure caps a week of pressure. On November 8, the board had announced an investigation into Huang's relationship with a former Megaspeed executive, as reported [on November 9](https://dobleclick.jaguridi.cl/en/2026/11/09/nvidia-investiga-relacion-huang-posible-espia-china.html). According to the Wall Street Journal, cited [on November 12](https://dobleclick.jaguridi.cl/en/2026/11/12/aws-abre-region-nube-chile-sin-plataformas-ia.html), the board was preparing to remove him. Huang also agreed to testify behind closed doors before Congress ahead of Thanksgiving, on November 26. That was stated by Brian Mast, chair of the Foreign Affairs Committee, according to [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/12/jensen-huang-agrees-to-testify-before-congress/), which cites Bloomberg and NBC.
