What is new today changes the meaning of the story: a senior White House official confirmed that the cutoff of access to the Claude Fable 5 and Mythos 5 models is specific to Anthropic and will not be extended to other labs, and that it was Amazon that alerted the administration to the alleged "jailbreak" of Mythos, as [revealed by The Information and reported by Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/06/13/anthropic-disables-fable-mythos-export-controls-national-security-threat/). The detail is uncomfortable, because that same Amazon hosts Fable 5 on its Bedrock platform and lost the service along with the rest of the world. Let us recall the context: since June 12, an order from the US Department of Commerce has forced Anthropic to switch off those two models for the entire planet, in the first case of a government using an export control as an off switch on a frontier AI model.

That the ban is aimed at a single company, rather than an even-handed rule for the whole industry, moves the question from the terrain of national security to that of corporate geopolitics. No one has publicly explained what incentives Amazon —Anthropic's compute provider— had in warning about a vulnerability in a model it hosts on its own cloud. Anthropic, for its part, maintains that the standard applied, if generalized, "would halt all frontier model deployments across the entire industry." The ban remains in place with no announced timeline for resolution, and the international reaction has been strong: executives and policy analysts accuse Washington of turning access to AI into a tool of geopolitical pressure. For Latin America the reading is direct: the region lost access not because of a universal policy, but as collateral damage from a targeted political decision on which it was not consulted.
