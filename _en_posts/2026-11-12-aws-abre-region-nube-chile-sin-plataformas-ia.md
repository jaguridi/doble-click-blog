## In 60 seconds

- **What happened.** On November 11, Amazon Web Services opened its first cloud region in Chile, in early access and with banks among its first customers.
- **Why it matters.** Chilean companies will be able to store and process data in the country, but the AI platforms Bedrock and SageMaker will arrive only during the first year.
- **What we don't know yet.** The date when those platforms will be added, and whether the region will reach general availability by the end of 2026, as the company expects.

BancoEstado, BTG, Cencosud, Consorcio, Itaú, and Ripley are the first customers of the new region, according to [Amazon's announcement](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/aws/aws-south-america-chile-region-launch). A region is a set of data centers that lets a company host its systems inside the country. Chile's is the third region in Latin America for Amazon Web Services (AWS), Amazon's cloud division. It starts with compute, storage, networking, and databases. The company says its centers are cooled mainly with air.

The economic figures come from Amazon itself. In addition to the investment, the company estimates a contribution of more than $7.5 billion to Chile's gross domestic product (GDP). It also projects an average of 4,100 full-time equivalent jobs per year. These are the company's projections about its own project, not independent assessments.

Amazon Bedrock and SageMaker AI, AWS services for working with AI models, will arrive during the first year. Until then, a bank that wants to use them might have to do so from AWS regions in other countries. That suggests that, for now, the Chilean region settles where data is stored and where general compute runs, but not yet where the models run.

## In the region

In Chile, the Ministry of Science (MinCiencia) and the Chamber of Deputies created a Permanent Data Center Roundtable, with the Ministry of Economy as its secretariat. It will meet every week starting November 16, according to [DPL News](https://dplnews.com/minciencia-diputados-mesa-data-centers-chile/). This is a press report; no official announcement was found.

In Brazil, Aguinaldo Ribeiro is the rapporteur of the AI bill (PL 2.338/2023). He proposes separating AI policy from the enforcement of regulation, currently in the hands of the National Data Protection Authority (ANPD). According to [JOTA](https://www.jota.info/legislativo/relator-do-pl-da-ia-quer-reducao), he wants to bring it to a vote before the end of the year.
