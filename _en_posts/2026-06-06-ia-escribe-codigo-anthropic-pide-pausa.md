On June 4, Jack Clark and Marina Favaro of the Anthropic Institute published ["When AI Builds Itself"](https://www.anthropic.com/institute/recursive-self-improvement): a call to create a verifiable global mechanism that would allow labs to pause in a coordinated way if AI reaches the ability to improve itself recursively. The central argument for urgency is concrete: Claude already writes more than 80% of the code in Anthropic's own codebase, and its engineers merge eight times more code per day than two years ago.

The proposal comes with context that deserves attention. Four days earlier, Anthropic filed a confidential S-1 with the SEC, and eight days earlier it closed its $65 billion Series H at a valuation of 965 billion. The structural conflict of interest is hard to ignore: the company that built one of the most capable AI systems in the world is asking everyone to stop at the same time, but only if everyone stops. For Latin America, the problem runs deeper: if such an agreement were ever negotiated, the region was not present in Glasswing, has no representation in CAISI, and was not consulted in any of last week's three governance events (Trump's executive order, the European package and the Great American AI Act).

## In the region

Colombia enters the final stretch of its presidential campaign with the most fragile regulatory situation in the region: Law 2502, which defines AI impersonation as an offense, was approved, but its enforcement mechanisms only take effect in July, ten days after the June 21 vote. Without a body equivalent to the TSE with technical capacity in AI, and with voice cloning available for as little as $10 a month, the election arrives with [active deepfakes already documented](https://www.areacucuta.com/elecciones-presidenciales-en-colombia-este-domingo-alerta-ante-la-proliferacion-de-deepfakes-y-desinformacion-con-ia/). According to AtlasIntel, Espriella leads with 50.3% against Cepeda's 42.6%. It will be the first high-visibility test in the region of whether it is possible to defend an electoral process from generative disinformation without operational regulatory tools. At the global level, the week's triple governance event consolidates two incompatible models: the European one, with obligations and data sovereignty; and the American one, with voluntary disclosures and state preemption. For Latin American regulators, the benchmark regulatory standard will remain the EU AI Act.
