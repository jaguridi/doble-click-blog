## In 60 seconds

- **What happened.** On November 12, the Ibero-American Data Protection Network published standards on how to use personal data when training and operating AI systems.
- **Why it matters.** A bank or a ministry that buys AI from a provider remains accountable for the data processing whose purposes it decides itself.
- **What we don't know yet.** Whether any national data authority will cite these criteria when ruling on a complaint, because the text offers guidance and does not replace the laws.

The fact that a piece of data is in plain view of anyone is not enough, on its own, to use it to train an AI system. So say the [Ibero-American Standards on the Use of Personal Data in Artificial Intelligence Systems](https://www.redipd.org/documento/estandares-iberoamericanos-sobre-uso-de-datos-personales-en-sistemas-ia.pdf), from the Ibero-American Data Protection Network (RIPD). The network brings together national data protection authorities. The document also extends the protection of sensitive data to inferences: what a system deduces about someone's health, ideology, or sexual orientation.

The text also sets out rights for individuals. They must be able to know whether their data was used and be able to challenge an automated decision with real human intervention. It declares incompatible the systems that manipulate deceptively and those that carry out discriminatory social scoring. The unanimous approval is recorded in the [network's statement](https://www.redipd.org/noticias/ripd-aprueba-estandares-internacionales-pioneros-sobre-ia-y-proteccion-datos-personales).

The rule with the most direct effect could be the one on the supplier chain. According to the Standards, whoever uses a third-party system remains responsible for the processing whose purposes it decides. For an entity that contracts someone else's model, that suggests responsibility for the data is not transferred with the contract. That criterion could make its way into public procurement specifications. Its real weight will depend on national authorities using it when they enforce their own laws.

## In the region

In Paraguay, [TEDIC](https://www.tedic.org/caso-ueno-biometria-datos-personales-y-la-deuda-del-estado-paraguayo/) is calling for a moratorium on facial recognition after the collapse of UENO Bank. The bank validated identities with video selfies from a company in its own group. It was placed in resolution on November 2 by decision of the Central Bank, according to the November 12 analysis. The organization points out that Law 7593 on personal data, enacted on November 27, 2025, takes effect 24 months after its publication. It also calls for public consultation on its implementing regulations.
