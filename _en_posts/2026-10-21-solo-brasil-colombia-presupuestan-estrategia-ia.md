## In 60 seconds

- **What happened.** Chile's CENIA center and the Economic Commission for Latin America presented an AI index that measures 20 countries and is topped by Chile.
- **Why it matters.** Governments with an AI strategy but no budget, Chile included, do not show how they would pay for the compute that the index finds scarce.
- **What we don't know yet.** Whether Chile and the other countries without a defined amount will allocate funds to their strategy, and on what timeline.

The index's widest gap is in compute. The five countries outside the region used for comparison have an average of 244.7 GPUs per million inhabitants, according to [the index's website](https://indicelatam.cl/). In the 20 Latin American and Caribbean countries measured, the figure is 8.5. GPUs are the processors used to train and run AI models, and Brazil holds 87.7% of those in the region.

The Latin American Artificial Intelligence Index (ILIA) is produced by Chile's National Center for Artificial Intelligence (CENIA) and ECLAC. Its fourth edition was presented on October 20, as [Doble Click anticipated a day earlier](https://dobleclick.jaguridi.cl/en/2026/10/20/agentes-ia-atacan-falla-contencion-pedido-militar.html). Chile scores 71.4 points, followed by Brazil (68.4) and Uruguay (67.0), while Mexico came in eighth, with 52.0, according to [CENIA's press release](https://cenia.cl/2026/10/20/ilia-2026-4ta-edicion-revela-que-america-latina-y-el-caribe-se-juegan-su-futuro-en-ia-en-la-colaboracion-regional-para-datos-talento-y-computo/). Among companies, AI reaches 27.6% of large firms, 12.8% of medium-sized ones and 6.8% of small ones.

Of the countries, 85% have a national AI strategy in force or in preparation, but only Brazil and Colombia specify how much money they will devote to carrying it out. Chile is in the other group: it leads the ranking without a defined amount. Without an allocated budget line, a congress would have nothing to debate in the budget law, and a ministry would have no spending target to be held accountable against. The GPU gap suggests where that absence could weigh most, because compute requires large and sustained purchases.
