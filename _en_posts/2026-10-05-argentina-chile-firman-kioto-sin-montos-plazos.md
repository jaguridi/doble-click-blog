## In 60 seconds

- **What happened.** Seventeen countries, among them Argentina and Chile, endorsed in Kyoto a United States declaration to expand their scientists' access to superintelligence tools.
- **Why it matters.** Because the text sets no amounts or deadlines, its effect for researchers will depend on what each signatory government later decides on its own.
- **What we don't know yet.** Whether Argentina or Chile will announce concrete measures so their researchers can use compute and scientific data after this signing.

The declaration takes up one page and does not use the term artificial intelligence. It says "Super Intelligence (SI)," according to [the text published by the White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Kyoto-Vision-for-a-Golden-Age-of-Science.pdf). The change comes from [Executive Order 14434](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-10-02/pdf/2026-20321.pdf), signed on September 29, which replaced one term with the other in the US federal government. For anyone reading the document, superintelligence here refers to what used to be called AI.

The endorsement came on October 4, at the annual meeting of the Science and Technology in Society forum, according to [the White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/releases/2026/10/us-leads-international-coalition-to-endorse-kyoto-vision-for-a-golden-age-of-science/). The initiative was promoted by Michael Kratsios, director of the Office of Science and Technology Policy. On the list of signatories, Argentina and Chile are the only Latin American countries. Brazil and Mexico do not appear.

The verb matters. According to the text, the signatories "will seek" to expand researchers' access to superintelligence tools, scientific data, compute and experimental facilities. The White House statement, by contrast, says the ministers committed. With no amounts or deadlines, the signing does not bind the governments of Argentina and Chile to anything measurable. Its reach might only become visible in the decisions they make about compute and data for their own scientists.
