## In 60 seconds

- **What happened.** Attorneys general from 41 U.S. states and the District of Columbia objected in Delaware to the sale of Character.AI's assets, chats included.
- **Why it matters.** If the judge approves without conditions, the buyer might not be bound by the privacy promise under which the company gathered that data.
- **What we don't know yet.** Who will win the asset auction and under what conditions the Delaware judge will approve the sale of user data.

The sale includes the chat histories of more than 21 million U.S. accounts, with metadata such as the IP address, which identifies the connection from which the messages were written. More than 2.1 million of those accounts were registered by minors, according to the [coalition's filing](https://oag.ca.gov/system/files/attachments/press-docs/characterai.pdf) of November 20 before the bankruptcy court. The baseline bidder is Smirks and Pangs Acquisition LLC: its offer sets the floor for the auction. That company was formed the day before Character.AI chose it and would pay $10 million in cash. The company is also asking for approval of a $350,000 breakup fee for that bidder. According to the [California attorney general's statement](https://oag.ca.gov/news/press-releases/attorney-general-bonta-opposes-asset-sale-characterai-amid-child-safety-concerns) of November 24, the auction will be held on December 1.

The coalition objects that the sale would leave behind the conditions under which that data was collected. According to its filing, Character.AI promised not to sell personal data, and a buyer approved without restrictions would not be bound by that promise. It therefore asks, at a minimum, that minors' chats and other sensitive data be excluded from the transaction.

There is also a question of timing. According to the coalition, the rules of California, Colorado, New York and Connecticut for companion chatbots take effect on January 1, 2027. Whoever closes the purchase in December would inherit the platform weeks before those obligations. That suggests the court is deciding more than a price: it could also define who will answer to those rules once they apply.

## In the region

In Argentina, the [Chief of Cabinet Office's Report 146](https://www.senado.gob.ar/prensa/adjunto/descargarArchivo/4286) was presented in the Senate on November 25. It sets criteria for the AI of the future state cybersecurity operations center: human oversight, auditability and a ban on training third-party models with state data. The center is funded with $15 million in non-reimbursable funds from the United States and a 30 million loan from the Inter-American Development Bank (IDB). The report also says that the state agent MIA uses Meta's Llama model.
