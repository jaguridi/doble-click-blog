**Doble Click** is a daily blog about artificial intelligence from a Latin American perspective. Every day we gather the most relevant things that happened in AI (launches, research, governance, industry moves) and tell them in a single post, written for an informed general audience.

On Mondays it is joined by **[Doble Lectura](/en/doble-lectura/)**: a weekly analysis of a paper or a public policy report, read slowly. First what the document says, and then what it means from the region. They are two paces of the same thing: the daily post moves with the news and the reading stops to dwell on one text. You get both if you subscribe to the newsletter.

The focus is **the region**: what in all of this matters for Latin America, what opportunities it opens up and which conversations are worth having.

## How it is made {#como-se-hace}

Posts are generated **automatically** from public sources (official lab blogs, universities, multilateral bodies, specialized press and the region's regulators) and always link to the original source so you can dig deeper.

## About this English edition {#edicion-en-ingles}

Doble Click is written in Spanish. This English edition is a **translation of the Spanish original**, also done automatically with Claude, Anthropic's models: every post and every Doble Lectura has its counterpart, with the same sources and links. When the Spanish original is corrected, the translation is updated to match. If a translation says something the original doesn't, the Spanish version is the one that counts, and we would like to hear about it through the error form.

The newest post may take a few hours to appear in English: until then, it is only available in Spanish. The audio summaries are Spanish-only for now.

## Which sources we follow

We look at a wide range of sources, some every day and others in a weekly sweep: **frontier AI labs**, **universities and research centers in Latin America**, the region's **ministries and regulators**, **multilateral bodies** and standards, model **safety and evaluation** teams, **analysis and industry**, the **Latin American press** and **curated research**.

That list is **representative, not exhaustive**: it shows what kind of source we pay attention to, with examples from each category, but the internal curation is broader and changes with what comes up. Think something is missing? **[Suggest a source →](/en/sources/#sugerir)**: we'll review it and, if it fits, add it.

## An important caveat

Posts are published **without prior human review**. We do our best to be accurate and always link to the source, but there may be errors of interpretation or summary. This blog is best taken as a starting point, not a definitive source: before citing a story or making decisions based on it, check the link to the original source.

## Who is responsible {#responsable}

The project is run by **José Guridi**, a researcher. Doble Click is a personal experiment in editorial automation: there is no newsroom, team or institution behind it.

**What is automated.** Practically everything you see: the daily curation of sources, the writing of the posts and of Doble Lectura (with Claude, Anthropic's models), the translation into English, the audio for each piece (synthetic voice) and the newsletter delivery. No one writes or approves each text before it goes out.

**What oversight there is.** A periodic review, not one before publication. Each Doble Lectura also goes through a second automated review, against the original document, before the Monday email goes out. And the reports readers leave are processed every night: when an error is verified, the page is corrected and a **correction note is added at the bottom**, so the correction is visible and not hidden.

**How an error gets corrected.** At the end of every post and every reading there is a **"Spotted an error?"** form. It is the fastest route and the one that leaves a record: tell us what's wrong, quoting the sentence if you can and, if you have it, the source that corrects it. Corrections are made to the Spanish original and then carried over to the translation.

**How to get in touch.** For anything else (a missing source, a question, a complaint that isn't about an error): [j.guridi.b@gmail.com](mailto:j.guridi.b@gmail.com).

## Newsletter

Before it starts we send you a single email to confirm, and your subscription begins when you click it. **For now the newsletter goes out in Spanish only**; an English edition is on the way.

## Privacy {#privacidad}

Your email is stored in a **private Google spreadsheet (Sheets)** managed by the person who runs this blog. There is nothing else behind it: no marketing platform, no profiling, no tracking of who opens what.

We use it **exclusively** to send you the newsletter. We don't share it with anyone or use it for anything else.

To unsubscribe, every email has an **unsubscribe** link at the bottom: when you click it, your address is deleted from the spreadsheet. You don't have to write to anyone or give any explanation.

**Site statistics.** Visits are counted with [GoatCounter](https://www.goatcounter.com/), an open-source counter that uses no cookies and stores no data that identifies readers. Besides page views, it records a few actions: whether someone reached the end of a post, played the audio, opened a source or submitted a form. It never records what you type: not your email, not the text of a report, not what you search for in the archive. It helps us know what gets read and what to improve, not who reads it.
