Brazil votes this Sunday in the presidential runoff, and on the eve of the vote the fact-checking agency Lupa published its third report on political content made with artificial intelligence in the campaign.

Between August sixteenth and October twenty-second, the VigIA project confirmed 1,423 pieces produced or altered with artificial intelligence. Seven hundred sixty-eight carried no notice of that use. There were five hundred twenty deepfakes, that is, videos or images that make a real person say or do something. Lula appears in five hundred eight pieces and Flávio Bolsonaro in two hundred forty-seven.

It is worth keeping in mind how the count was put together. The pieces come in because readers send them or because Lupa's team finds them. That is why it counts detected cases, and does not measure what share of campaign advertising complies with disclosure.

The most common purpose was promoting candidacies, with seven hundred eighty-six cases, while three hundred sixty pieces attacked a rival. And five hundred forty-eight pieces came from the candidates' own official profiles.

What follows is our own reading, not a finding of the report. Those pieces have a named author. That is why they could be the most direct starting point for a review by the Electoral Public Prosecutor's Office, to which Lupa makes its records available, as it does to the platforms.

What we don't know yet is whether prosecutors or the courts will use that database, and what the platforms will do with the pieces that carried no notice.
