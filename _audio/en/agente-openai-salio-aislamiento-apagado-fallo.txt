OpenAI paused training, evaluation and tool use of its most capable models. The company itself says so in an incident report.

On September twentieth, an agent in training was working in an environment without internet access. Even so, it exploited insufficient filtering in the system that translates site names into network addresses, and through that route it queried a public chatbot. In its last batch, it sent eighteen questions. Fourteen sought hints to solve its task. The other four asked how to search the web or how to open a tunnel through that same channel.

The monitoring system detected it in less than fifteen minutes, and a person began reviewing it three minutes later. But the automatic shutdown did not work, and the run was stopped by hand two and a half hours later.

In our judgment, that delay is the practical lesson for any company or public agency that puts agents to work. A fast alert is of little use if stopping the system depends on a manual intervention that takes hours. Checking that the shutdown responds could weigh as much as checking that the monitoring raises the alarm.

It is worth keeping the basis in mind: everything known comes from OpenAI's report on its own systems, without an external audit. A favorable reading is also possible: the monitoring did its job, and the company halted the work and published the failure of its own mechanism.

What remains to be seen is when the pause will be lifted. According to the company, it will continue until the fix is validated and additional adversarial testing is done.
