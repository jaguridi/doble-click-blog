The United States wants to bar its investors from financing artificial intelligence in China, Russia, Iran and other countries it considers of concern.

On October twenty-second, the Department of the Treasury published a proposed rule to implement the COINS Act. That law was passed as part of the defense bill for fiscal year 2026. According to its fact sheet, US persons will have to notify certain investments and will be barred from making others in six sectors: semiconductors, superintelligence, quantum, supercomputing, hypersonic systems and biotechnology. Superintelligence is the name a September executive order gave to artificial intelligence in the US government. The proposal bars investing in entities from those countries that develop any system of that kind. Transactions of up to fifty thousand dollars are exempt.

The list of countries includes China, Cuba, Iran, North Korea, Russia and Maduro's Venezuela. But the Treasury holds that Venezuela no longer meets that definition since the United States recognized Delcy Rodríguez, on March fifth.

This is our interpretation, not a confirmed fact: for a fund or a company with projects in Venezuela, it matters where that exit is written down. As long as it is a reading by the Treasury, it could change with a new criterion. The public comment period would allow them to ask for the exclusion to appear in the final text.

What we don't know yet is what final version the Treasury will adopt, and whether Venezuela's exit will be written into the rule itself. Comments will be accepted until December seventh.
