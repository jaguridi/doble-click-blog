The World Bank published its semiannual report on Latin America and the Caribbean on October sixth, devoted to artificial intelligence and productivity. It projects growth of two point two percent for the region in 2026, somewhat less than the two point four percent of 2025.

According to the bank, a median of seventeen percent of working-age adults say they use generative artificial intelligence tools. That is about half the level in the United States and Canada. It is worth noting that this is use reported by each person. Among companies, the bank describes broad adoption that rarely reaches their core processes.

The report also sorts workers by type of job. About eight percent hold skilled jobs that this technology could enhance. Around ten percent work in routine cognitive tasks, the most exposed to automation. And about a quarter hold routine manual jobs that, according to the bank, a second wave could reach.

For the bank, the main barriers are management capacity, skills and the reorganization of companies, not cost or access. What follows is an interpretation, not a conclusion of the report. If the bottleneck lies in how companies are organized, making licenses or connectivity cheaper might not be enough to raise productivity.

It remains to be seen whether labor and education ministries will use the division between jobs that can be enhanced and jobs that are exposed to decide what training they offer.
