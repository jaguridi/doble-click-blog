China proposes controlling the export of its large artificial intelligence models.

On October sixteenth, China's ministries of Commerce and of Science and Technology published a draft that adds forty technologies to its list of prohibited and restricted exports. Twenty-three are artificial intelligence: large models, agents, compute clusters and information processing.

According to the draft's annex, the weights, code and architecture of the largest models are restricted. The criterion considers the compute used to train them, a size of two trillion parameters or more, or a place among the top ten in public global rankings. Anything published as open source or in academic papers is excluded. In addition, starting October sixteenth, exporters must first register on a Ministry of Commerce platform.

The ministry's spokesperson clarified that the control does not reach the connections other programs use to query a model. Nor does it reach web applications or software used as an online service.

Our reading is this: someone who uses a Chinese model over the internet should not notice any changes. What could require a license is handing over the model itself, for example to a university or a ministry in another country that wants to process its data on its own servers. And publishing a model as open source could become the path with the least paperwork for distributing it outside China.

What we don't know yet is what will change in the final text and from when it would apply. The public consultation accepts comments until November fifteenth.
