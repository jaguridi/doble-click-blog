Brazil's electoral justice system is adding forensic experts to review content made with artificial intelligence.

Brazil's Superior Electoral Court created a Digital Forensics Unit. Its task is to examine images, audio, and videos suspected of having been fabricated or manipulated, especially with artificial intelligence. The rule that creates it was published in the judiciary's official journal on Thursday, October eighth, according to the court itself.

The unit will not review everything. It will act on an exceptional basis, depending on the severity, scale, or potential harm of each case. Beyond the pieces themselves, it can analyze files and metadata, as well as signs of automation, mass messaging, and coordinated inauthentic behavior, according to CNN Brasil. Automated tools only assist: specialists interpret the results.

According to the court, every report will have to set out the materials, methods, results, limitations, and the people responsible. This is our interpretation, not a fact: that format could change litigation. A campaign's defense that objects to an expert report would have before it a document with its method and its limits in writing, which it can contest point by point. At the same time, since the use is exceptional, a large share of complaints would continue to be resolved without this analysis.

One question remains open. The rule provides for a list of collaborating experts and institutions, with confidentiality rules. The announcement does not detail what criteria the court will use to add universities and outside experts.

The next milestone is October twenty-fifth, when Brazil votes in the presidential runoff.
