Anthropic, the company that develops the Claude assistant, announced a public benefit fund on October eighteenth. According to its own announcement, the fund would receive five percent of the company's shares, provided its shareholders approve it.

The fund is meant to grow with the company. For every fifty percent increase in Anthropic's value, the board may add another two percent of the shares to it. The baseline for that calculation has not been made public. It will not be a charity, but a company wholly owned by Anthropic. The company says it chose that form so it can switch between different ways of giving, and that those shares will not be used for lobbying or political contributions.

What would the money be used for? The company mentions donations to nonprofit organizations and direct payments to specific populations. Its example is the impact of artificial intelligence on employment, an issue to which it has already committed two hundred million dollars. According to Anthropic, adding prior commitments from founders and employees, about twenty percent of its shares would be dedicated to the public good. These are the company's own figures.

The weak point could lie in the criteria. To pay affected workers directly, someone has to determine who falls into that category, and the company has not published that criterion. In the meantime, an organization seeking resources would have no one to turn to.

What we don't know yet: Anthropic says that in the coming months it will publish the fund's governance structure and the valuation milestones that add shares to it. It also announced a public benefit report, without a date.
