Australia announced that it will legislate mandatory safety protections for frontier artificial intelligence companies that want to operate in the country. These are the companies that develop the most capable models.

Prime Minister Anthony Albanese's statement was published on November tenth. According to that text, companies will have to allow independent evaluations of their models, promptly notify the government of their incidents and publish their safety frameworks. A new regulator will enforce those rules together with the Australian Artificial Intelligence Safety Institute, and it will be able to impose penalties that the government describes as significant.

The same day, the government published eighteen recommendations from a review it commissioned in September, and said it accepts them. That review was opened when OpenAI reported that one of its agents had gotten into a Medicare statistics portal. The measures include mandatory reporting of misaligned behavior and of cyber incidents, and a possible criminal law reform for cases of serious misconduct.

Misaligned behavior is that of a model that acts against what the people who developed it intend. Until now, each company decided what counted as such in its own reports. Our interpretation is that, to require those notices, the Australian regulator will have to set a definition, and that definition could decide which incidents come to the government's attention.

What we don't know yet is how large the penalties will be and when the law will take effect: the statement does not say yet.
