An attacker used Claude agents against health organizations in Argentina. That is what the cybersecurity firm CrowdStrike reports, in an investigation published on its blog on October twenty-first.

According to the company, four actors that had not been reported before used artificial intelligence agents in real intrusions between May and August of this year. One of them operates in Spanish and English. It compromised at least fifteen health organizations based in Argentina, and from at least one of them it took credentials and medical records.

To do this, it built its own system with two Claude Code agents. A supervisor agent chose the next task and another, the executor, carried it out. Another of the actors, a Chinese-speaking one, attacked private companies in Brazil and the United States. It reached the Claude models through an intermediary service based in China.

It is worth keeping in mind where the information comes from. All of these details come from CrowdStrike, and what has been published does not include independent verification.

The firm also did not name the Argentine organizations or confirm which ones were notified, according to the specialized newsletter Dark News, which published the case on October twenty-fifth. As a result, the report does not let patients know whether their medical records got out. In Argentina, the Agency for Access to Public Information handles complaints and keeps the registry of those who violate the personal data protection law. A complaint to that agency could be the way for a patient to find out whether their information was exposed.

What we don't know yet is which organizations were attacked, whether they were notified, and whether any reported the incident to that authority.
