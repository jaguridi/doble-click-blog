The ministers and senior digital transformation authorities of the Ibero-American countries approved the Ibero-American Artificial Intelligence Alliance on November third, in Madrid. The declaration refers it to the heads of state. Two footnotes in the document say that Argentina and Paraguay dissociate themselves from the text.

According to the declaration, the Alliance has three parts. The first is a common data space, with a federated architecture: each institution keeps its own data and the network connects them. The second is language models in Spanish, in Portuguese and, progressively, in Indigenous languages. The third is cooperation on high-performance computing, that is, the large servers needed to train homegrown models. Joining will be voluntary.

According to the newspaper Infobae, the Ibero-American Summit closed on November fifth with the Alliance approved and with Argentina and Paraguay outside it. The same outlet says that the initiative seeks to connect four projects: ALIA, from Spain; AMALIA, from Portugal; Latam GPT, from Chile; and SoberanIA, from Brazil.

What follows is an interpretation, not a fact from the document. For Latam GPT, the compute part could be the one that matters most. If the Alliance sets rules for access to that shared capacity, those rules could determine how much compute its next versions are trained with.

What we don't know yet is which governments will formally join, and under what criteria that capacity will be divided among the projects. In addition, the Summit's final text was not available on the website of the Ibero-American General Secretariat as of November seventh.
