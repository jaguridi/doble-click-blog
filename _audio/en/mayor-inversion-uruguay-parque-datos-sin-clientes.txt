Uruguay's president, Yamandú Orsi, signed an agreement on October fourteenth with the Spanish company Solaria Energía y Medio Ambiente. It is a five hundred megawatt data center park, according to a statement from the Uruguayan Presidency.

The investment is 5.7 billion dollars. Of that, 5.5 billion goes to the data centers and 200 million to the park's own solar plant of three hundred fifty megawatts. The government presents it as the largest investment the country will receive in its history. The park will operate as a free trade zone, with facilities in Canelones, San José and Florida.

The project has two phases. The first brings together two hundred megawatts of data centers and all of the solar generation. The second adds another three hundred megawatts of data centers. The government estimates 3,100 construction jobs through 2029 and 1,500 permanent ones once the project is complete.

Our interpretation, which is not in the statement, is this: once the park is finished, its capacity would exceed that of its solar plant by one hundred fifty megawatts. If it needs more power, it would have to connect to the grid of Uruguay's state-owned power company. How much electricity it would draw, and on what terms, could define the project's weight for the public grid.

What we don't know yet is who will use that capacity. The official statement, reviewed on October fifteenth, does not name any customer.
