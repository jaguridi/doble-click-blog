Seventeen countries endorsed on October fourth, in Kyoto, a declaration promoted by the United States called the Kyoto Vision for a Golden Age of Science. Argentina and Chile are the only Latin American countries on the list, according to the White House. Brazil and Mexico do not appear.

The text takes up one page and uses the word superintelligence where artificial intelligence used to be said. That change of word comes from an executive order signed in Washington on September twenty-ninth, which replaced one term with the other in the US federal government.

The key is the verb. According to the document, the signatories will seek to expand researchers' access to superintelligence tools, scientific data, computing capacity and experimental facilities. The White House statement goes further and says the ministers committed. But the declaration sets no amounts or deadlines.

One possible interpretation is that the signing does not bind Argentina or Chile to anything measurable. Its effect might only become visible in what each government decides about compute and data for its scientists.
