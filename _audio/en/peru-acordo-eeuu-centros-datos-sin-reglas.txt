Peru and the United States signed a memorandum of understanding on artificial intelligence in September. Its text had not been made public until now. The Peruvian outlet OjoPúblico reported on November eighth that it had obtained exclusive access to the document.

According to OjoPúblico, the agreement contemplates building digital infrastructure in Peru, including data centers. It also seeks to facilitate commercial and business activities between US artificial intelligence providers and the Peruvian public and private sectors.

The newspaper El Peruano had reported the signing on September eighth. According to that newspaper, it was signed by the President of the Council of Ministers, Luis Galarreta, and Ambassador Bernie Navarro. The memorandum covers regulation, cloud, data centers, fifth generation mobile networks, cybersecurity and training, and is in force for three years. The Peruvian point of contact is the Secretariat of Government and Digital Transformation.

OjoPúblico adds that Peru has no specific regulation on data centers or on their possible environmental impacts. This is our interpretation, not a fact from the sources. If projects arrive during those three years, they could be evaluated with general permits alone, unless Congress first approves a rule of its own.

It remains to be seen which specific projects will be promoted under the agreement and whether the Peruvian government will publish the official text.
