Jensen Huang no longer runs Nvidia. He resigned on November twelfth, effective immediately, as chair, as chief executive officer and from the company's board, according to the company's own announcement.

Colette Kress, chief financial officer since 2013, took over as interim chief executive officer and keeps her finance duties until a successor is named. The board says it will look for candidates inside and outside the company, and it named Harvey Jones, who previously ran the company Synopsys, as its first chair.

The departure comes after a week of pressure. On November eighth, the board had opened an investigation. It is examining Huang's relationship with a former executive of the company Megaspeed.

On that same November twelfth, Senator Ron Wyden and Representative Richard Neal, both Democrats, wrote to the United States Commerce Department. They asked it to preserve all its records on Nvidia's chip sales to China, including those on licenses and tariffs. Our interpretation is that, if those documents are preserved and handed over, the discussion could shift from Nvidia's leadership to how the government authorized those sales. For now it is only a possibility.

In addition, according to TechCrunch, which cites Bloomberg and NBC, Huang agreed to testify behind closed doors before Congress before November twenty-sixth.

What we don't know yet: who the successor will be and what Nvidia will say on November seventeenth, when it presents its quarterly results.
