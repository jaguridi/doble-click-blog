Chile's Office of the Comptroller General of the Republic asked the Ministry of Science to explain the memorandum called OpenAI for Chile. Minister Ximena Lincolao and Sam Altman signed it on November sixteenth in San Francisco.

The official letter is dated November eighteenth and was made public on the twentieth, according to the outlet BioBioChile. It gives ten business days to deliver a report. That deadline runs from the moment the letter completes its processing, so there is still no exact expiration date.

The Comptroller is asking for the full text of the memorandum, its duration, the obligations the state takes on and whether there are financial commitments. It also asks about the expenses of the minister's trip and about the memorandum with the company ElevenLabs. The request responds to a petition from Deputy Jaime Araya. It is a request for information: for now, the Comptroller is not ruling on the legality of the agreement.

After the signing, the ministry had said the agreement would be free of charge and non-exclusive, and that it would not publish the document. Now it will have to hand it over to the oversight body.

One of the questions could have the greatest reach. The ministry must say whether the memorandum is subject to public procurement rules. This is this blog's own reading: if those rules apply, any later use of OpenAI tools in state services might have to go through them.

The letter also asks how personal data are protected, including transfers to other countries. And it requests similar agreements the ministry signed with companies since March eleventh, twenty twenty-six.

What we don't know yet is how the ministry will respond and whether the Comptroller will later issue a ruling.
