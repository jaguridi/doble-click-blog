Character Technologies, the company that owns the chatbot platform Character dot ai, started a voluntary bankruptcy process in the United States on October twelfth. It did so under what is known as Chapter eleven, which allows assets to be sold under a judge's supervision. It wants to sell all or substantially all of its assets, and the case is in the Delaware bankruptcy court.

According to Bloomberg Law, the bid that sets the floor of the auction comes from Disney: twenty million dollars plus transition services. In exchange, Disney would receive a perpetual, non-exclusive license to certain intellectual property and certain rights of the company. Other buyers can top that bid.

The company, in its own announcement, attributes the decision to legal, regulatory and operational difficulties, without going into detail. It expects to close the sale in late November or early December. If no bid preserves the service, it will shut it down. For now the platform is running and paid subscriptions remain active.

Our reading is this: Disney's offer buys the right to use certain assets, but not the app or its operation. For anyone who pays for a subscription or keeps conversations there, that could mean the service's continuity depends on another buyer coming forward who wants to run it. In the meantime, the company reminds users that each of them can download or delete their information.

What we don't know yet is exactly which of the company's rights would be part of the license. It is also unclear whether any improved bid will include keeping the platform open.
