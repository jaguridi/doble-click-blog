On September twenty-ninth, Montevideo hosted a ministerial summit of the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development, the OECD, devoted to inclusive innovation and artificial intelligence. The meeting ends on September thirtieth.

The organization's secretary-general, Mathias Cormann, spoke at the opening. He projected that artificial intelligence could raise labor productivity by between thirteen and fifty-three percent over the next decade in the Latin American member countries. He also said that the region's productivity per hour is equivalent to thirty-eight percent of the organization's average. Both figures were reported by the newspaper Ámbito, and the first is a projection by the OECD itself, not a measurement.

Uruguay and Costa Rica co-chair the organization's regional program. At the summit, Uruguay's economy minister, Gabriel Oddone, said that OECD membership is a path that Uruguay is beginning to discuss.

Why does it matter? Cormann's projection covers only the Latin American members, and Uruguay is not one yet. If that discussion moves forward, the host country could go from leading the regional program to joining the group these projections are aimed at. That is our own reading, not something the Uruguayan government has announced.

As for the range of thirteen to fifty-three percent, its breadth suggests that the effect of artificial intelligence is far from automatic.

What we don't know yet is what the ministers agree on during the second day, and whether Uruguay takes any formal step toward accession.
