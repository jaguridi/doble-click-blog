On October 26, Meta launched two stock offerings totaling 37.5 billion dollars.

According to the documents the company itself filed with the United States securities regulator, there is a main offering. That offering is 40 million common shares at 625 dollars each, for 25 billion. They are sold through forward contracts that Meta will settle no later than November 2027. Added to that are 12.5 billion in convertible preferred stock, with a dividend of 6.5 percent. Nvidia and AMD, both chipmakers, will invest 3 billion and 1 billion, according to their own announcements.

Meta links these offerings to its investment in artificial intelligence infrastructure, although the stated use of the proceeds is general corporate purposes. In the same announcement, the company projects capital expenditures of between 135 billion and 145 billion dollars this year. All of these figures come from the company and its suppliers, not from an independent assessment.

The largest tranche is still pending. Meta plans to sell about 60 billion more on the market once a new Class C stock begins trading, which still has no agreement or terms. Our interpretation is that the timeline for that stock could determine when Meta again uses stock to finance spending like this. And also how much its ownership is spread across more shares.

What we don't know yet is when that Class C stock will begin trading, and on what conditions.
