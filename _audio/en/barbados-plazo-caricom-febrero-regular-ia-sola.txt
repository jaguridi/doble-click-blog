Barbados has put a date on the Caribbean Community. Prime Minister Mia Mottley said her country will have its own framework to regulate artificial intelligence if the regional report does not arrive in February. That report is owed by a commission that Caribbean heads of government agreed on in July. She said it at the close of the summit on artificial intelligence safety that Barbados and the UN held on November twenty-third in Bridgetown. The date is reported by the newspaper Barbados Today.

At the summit, according to the UN's news service, Mottley put forward two ideas. One makes the company responsible when one of its programs that acts on its own attacks a Barbadian bank. The other would allow the government to suspend those models in an emergency.

At the same gathering, Secretary-General António Guterres reiterated his proposal for a multilateral framework to manage the risks of artificial intelligence, with independent oversight. According to the AFP news agency, he called for developing it within the next year.

Our interpretation is that a public deadline shifts the position of the rest of the Caribbean bloc. If Barbados legislates first, the other governments could debate the regional approach against a rule already underway. Both of Mottley's ideas could be set in a national law without waiting for a global agreement.

What we don't know yet is whether the commission will deliver on time.
