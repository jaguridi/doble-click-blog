Pope Leo the Fourteenth called on the states that will meet in Geneva in November to take concrete steps toward banning lethal autonomous weapons. He said so on October thirtieth at the Apostolic Palace, before a meeting on the future of artificial intelligence. That is what the speech published by the Holy See Press Office states.

The pope proposed five considerations. One is that decisions about human life, freedom and the use of force remain under effective, informed and accountable human control. He also rejected granting rights or legal personhood to artificial intelligence systems. That refusal suggests that, when harm occurs, the claim would still be directed at a person or at a named institution. And he called for international institutions with independent oversight, accountability and redress for harm.

The call comes on an open file. In September, 128 states concluded a framework in Geneva that binds no one. In that round, the United States and Russia blocked a treaty, and a declaration by thirty-nine countries, led by Brazil, called for negotiating a binding one. Because decisions in that forum are made by consensus, the pope's backing is not enough to lift a veto. That could give the Brazilian delegation an argument to demand that negotiations be opened.

What we don't know yet is what the Review Conference in November will do: whether it opens that mandate to negotiate a binding instrument or accepts another extension of the talks.
