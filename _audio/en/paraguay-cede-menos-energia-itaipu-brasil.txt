Paraguay is selling less and less Itaipú energy to Brazil. The Brazilian outlet JOTA reports it, with data from Brazil's Ministry of Mines and Energy. The Paraguayan energy sold in Brazil fell from twenty-two point two million megawatt hours in 2023 to ten point five million in 2025.

Over the same period, the dam's supply to Paraguay rose from nineteen point five to twenty-five point eight million megawatt hours. And between January and September of this year, payments to Paraguay for ceding energy fell by thirty-three million dollars. All of these figures come through the press.

JOTA links those figures to the review both governments are conducting of Annex C of the treaty, which sets its financial terms. The current rate, nineteen dollars and twenty-eight cents per kilowatt per month, is in effect until December. For 2027, sources in the Brazilian government are weighing one of around fifteen dollars, according to the outlet. JOTA adds to that calculation an artificial intelligence compute complex that Crusoe and Araico plan to operate from mid-2027, next to the Itaipú substation.

What follows is our own interpretation, not a fact. Paraguay's state-owned utility, ANDE, has already committed to deliver up to two hundred eighty-six megawatts to a compute center in Yguazú. Each contract like that with a large local consumer could reduce what Brazil receives through cession and what Paraguay collects as compensation.

What we don't know yet is what rate the two countries will set for 2027. The review has not yet concluded.
