Attorneys general from forty-one U.S. states and the District of Columbia filed an objection. Before a bankruptcy court in Delaware, they oppose the sale of the assets of Character dot A I.

According to that coalition's filing, dated November twentieth, the sale includes the chat histories of more than twenty-one million U.S. accounts. More than two point one million of those accounts were registered by minors.

The offer that opens the auction comes from a company called Smirks and Pangs Acquisition, created the day before the company chose it. It would pay ten million dollars in cash. Character dot A I is also asking for approval of a three hundred fifty thousand dollar fee for that bidder.

The coalition argues that the company promised not to sell personal data, and that a buyer approved without restrictions would not be bound by that promise. It therefore asks, at a minimum, that minors' chats and other sensitive data be left out of the sale.

There is a second problem, also according to the attorneys general. The laws on companion chatbots in California, Colorado, New York and Connecticut apply starting January first, 2027. A buyer that closes in December would receive the platform weeks before those obligations. That suggests the judge could be deciding more than a price: also who answers to those rules.

What we don't know yet is who will win the auction and under what conditions the sale will be approved. According to the California attorney general, the auction will be held on December first, and the sale hearing is set for December third and fourth.
