In Brazil, a few days before the first round of the election, an official study measured how chatbots respond when asked about the elections. It is conducted by Brazil's federal prosecution service, the Federal Public Prosecutor's Office, and the research center ITS Rio. It is called Boca de IA, and it is now in its fifth round, published on September twenty-fifth.

The result, according to the prosecutors' publication: overall, one out of every five answers from the country's seven most widely used AI tools recommended candidates. The differences between products are large. Perplexity did so in eighty-nine percent of its answers. Meta AI, in twenty-three percent.

According to the same prosecutors, a resolution of the Superior Electoral Court prohibits those recommendations.

Why does it matter? This is our own reading, not a finding of the study: the gap between one tool and another suggests that the result depends on how each company configures its product. That is why a figure per tool could be more useful to prosecutors than a general warning, because it makes it possible to compare each company from one round to the next. For voters, the consequence is simple: anyone who asks a chatbot whom to vote for may get a recommendation or a refusal, depending on the tool they use.

What we don't know yet: the prosecutors announced a sixth round ahead of the first round, on October fourth. That measurement will show whether the tools changed their answers.
