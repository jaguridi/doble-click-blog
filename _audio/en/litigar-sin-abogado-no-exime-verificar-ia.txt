The Supreme Court of Puerto Rico fined each of two siblings five hundred dollars. They were litigating without a lawyer in an inheritance dispute.

Víctor Manuel and Mercedes Velázquez Feliciano filed briefs with citations falsely attributed to the Supreme Court itself. They did so before three levels of the island's courts: the Court of First Instance, the Court of Appeals and the Supreme Court. According to the separate opinion of Justice Raúl Candelario López, joined by three other justices, there were seven documents with dozens of nonexistent citations. The resolution is dated October twenty-eighth, and the fine must be paid within thirty days.

The court rejected the appeal and issued a warning. Those who appear pro se, that is, without legal representation, must validate the content they generate with artificial intelligence. The diligence required is similar to that of lawyers. If they fail to do so, they face financial or other sanctions. The same opinion clarifies that using artificial intelligence is not, by itself, grounds for a sanction. And according to Candelario López, it is the first time the Supreme Court has ruled on this issue for litigants without a lawyer.

What follows is our interpretation, not part of the resolution. Puerto Rico's trial and appellate judges could now have a Supreme Court standard for sanctioning briefs with invented citations, without waiting for an additional rule. And for those who litigate on their own, checking every citation an artificial intelligence assistant delivers would become their responsibility.

It remains to be seen whether Puerto Rico's Judicial Branch will accompany this warning with guidance for people who litigate without a lawyer.
